==================================================================

Complete inventory of every gameplay change made by VanillaKFC, derived directly from the source (src/main/java/com/example/vanillakfc/**,
src/main/resources/resourcepack/**). It's a Paper server plugin (targets Paper API 26.3), not a client mod - it only affects servers running this
jar.

Where vanilla has a well-established, stable value, it's listed for comparison.

1. COMBAT & HOSTILE MOBS
==================================================================

Creepers (CreeperListener, CreeperBehaviorTask)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Perception & mobility: follow range 16->32 blocks; step height 0.6->1.2 (clears a full block without jumping); jump strength x1.5.
- Explosion radius: 3->4 (6->8 charged); vanilla's charged-doubling still applies on top.
- Natural spawning: only 35% of spawn attempts succeed (vanilla: 100%, solo). A successful spawn can add a 2nd creeper within 24 blocks (50% chance,
  capped at 2 total).
- Active chase: while targeting a player (and not frozen, see below), re-paths straight at them at speed factor 3.0. Throttled to once per 2 blocks of
  target movement or every 10 ticks, rather than every tick, to stop erratic path flip-flopping around obstacles.
- Short-term memory: after losing sight of its target, keeps walking toward its last known position for 3s if that spot is still within 16 blocks.
  Movement only - doesn't restore the actual target, so swelling/igniting still needs a genuine re-spot.
- Freeze-when-watched: while any player has unobstructed line of sight (40 blocks, ~90 degree cone, 9 hitbox sample points), movement speed is zeroed
  (can still turn to face them). "Any player" is literal and always has been - a bystander watching a creeper chase someone else freezes it just as
  dead as the person it is after. The creeper's own target has no special standing in the check.
- See-through blocks don't count as obstruction (as of 2026-09-20). The sight ray stops at anything with a collision box, so a player watching from
  inside a base - through a window, over a fence, behind iron bars - was not registering as watching at all, and the creeper carried on as if
  unobserved. Glass (all colours, tinted included), glass panes, iron bars, chains, fences and fence gates, scaffolding, ice, barriers and light
  blocks are now transparent to the check. Leaves, slabs, stairs and walls still block it - they really do hide what's behind them.
- Freezing stops the creeper outright, not just its steering (as of 2026-09-20). Zeroing the speed attribute leaves two things running: the momentum
  of a chase at speed factor 3.0, which friction takes the better part of a second to bleed off while the creeper keeps drifting toward its target,
  and the queued path, ready to resume the instant the freeze lifts. Both are now cleared on the tick the freeze goes on. Horizontal velocity is
  zeroed only on that transition, not every frozen tick, so a watched creeper can still be knocked back when hit.
- Fuse blocked while watched: can't start swelling (hit-trigger or proximity/LoS trigger) while watched, unless already mid-fuse (which continues
  unaffected).
- Flees cats within 12 blocks - unchanged vanilla behavior, just replicated (no exposed API to retune it).
- Charged creepers are exempt from all of the above - always treated as "unwatched."

Endermen (EndermanListener, EndermanDodgeSoundPackListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Can no longer pick up blocks (placement unaffected).
- Melee damage: flat -2.0 off vanilla's difficulty-scaled damage (Easy 4.5->2.5, Normal 7->5, Hard 10.5->8.5).
- Dodge: 50% chance to dodge any player melee hit entirely - teleports 3-7 blocks away, 65% biased into a 30 degree cone directly behind the attacker
  (custom sound on the biased case, vanilla teleport sound otherwise).
- Pillar-punish: a hit landed while the attacker is standing at least one block above the Enderman's feet has a 50% chance to trigger a counter
  instead - teleport directly behind the player, hit with knockback strong enough to knock them off their perch, teleport back ~0.4s later.

Skeletons (SkeletonListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Movement speed cut to x0.2 while actively targeting (stationary snipers instead of walking shooters).
- Follow range 16->32 blocks.
- Shot interval hard-locked to an exact tick count: 120 ticks (6s) on Easy/Normal, 100 ticks (5s) on Hard - vs vanilla's ~60/~40-tick cooldowns -
  tracked by server tick count so it can't drift under lag. Every draw that starts always ends in a fired arrow.
- Every arrow leaves at 4x vanilla's velocity, re-aimed straight at the target instead of vanilla's slow-speed arc (which, scaled up as-is, would sail
  high over the target's head).
- Never idle with a target in view: since frozen movement stops it from repositioning to keep sight, a lost target now falls back to whoever last
  damaged it (if visible), then the nearest visible player - only goes target-less if neither is actually in view.
- Damage flattened to a fixed 3 (Easy) / 4 (Normal) / 5 (Hard), for both arrows and melee, replacing vanilla's velocity-dependent damage (which the 4x
  shot speed would otherwise inflate well past normal).

Spiders (SpiderListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Movement speed x1.5 (vanilla base 0.3). Cave Spiders are a distinct entity type and untouched by this listener.

Iron Golems (IronGolemListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Drop nothing on death - vanilla's iron ingots (0-2, more with Looting), poppy chance, and any XP are all cleared.

Zombies (ZombiePodListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Every natural zombie spawn additionally spawns one extra zombie at the same spot (tagged so it isn't doubled again), roughly doubling natural pack
  density.

Drowned (DrownedTridentListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Tridents thrown by a Drowned deal half damage (8 -> 4 raw, before armor). Only the thrown projectile is affected, matched by its shooter - a
  Drowned's melee hit is untouched, and so is any trident thrown or swung by a player. Halving happens pre-armor, so an armoured target takes slightly
  more than half of what it used to (armor absorbs a smaller share of a weaker hit); unarmoured, it's exactly half.

All hostile mobs - pod spawn cooldown (PodSpawnCooldownListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- New mechanic: once any hostile mob naturally spawns, no hostile mob of any species may naturally spawn within 6 blocks of that X/Z column (any
  height) for the next 5 minutes. Spawner spawns, breeding, and spawn eggs are unaffected - only natural world spawns are gated. Scoped to Bukkit's
  broad Enemy interface (includes Slime, MagmaCube, Ghast, Shulker, Phantom, Hoglin), not just the narrower Monster interface.

Mace (MaceDamageListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Raw base damage (hit + crit + fall-distance smash bonus, before armor/resistance) is capped at 40.0. Vanilla's fall-scaling smash bonus is otherwise
  unbounded, so extreme fall setups (elytra + wind charges, etc.) could produce arbitrarily large hits.

Spear (SpearDamageListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- The charge-attack bonus (extra damage scaled by relative velocity between attacker and target) is capped at 5x whatever the hit would have dealt
  without it (base weapon damage, including Sharpness). Applies to all 7 tiers (Wood/Stone/Copper/Iron/Gold/Diamond/Netherite). Smite and Bane of
  Arthropods aren't factored into the reference damage the cap is based on, so a Smite/Bane spear's effective cap against its favored target is
  slightly tighter than fully vanilla-accurate.

Beds & Respawn Anchors (BedExplosionListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Wrong-dimension explosion power (beds in the Nether/End, respawn anchors outside the Nether) is reduced from vanilla's 5.0 down to 1.0 - the same
  power as a ghast fireball.

Lightning (LightningListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Adds a second, independent ~1-in-100,000-per-loaded-chunk-per-tick strike roll during thunderstorms, striking the highest block in a random column
  of that chunk - vanilla's own roll still fires alongside it, combining into roughly double vanilla's normal strike rate.
- Gated on both isThundering() and hasStorm() being true (not just the former) - separate flags that always agree under vanilla's natural cycle but
  could desync via a command or another plugin; requiring both closes off any way this roll could fire without an actual storm overhead.


2. DEATH, PROGRESSION & SLEEP
==================================================================

Death drops & XP loss (DeathDropListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Gear survives death: any sword, spear, pickaxe, axe, hoe, shovel, armor piece, shield, bow, crossbow, trident, elytra, or mace is pulled from the
  death-drop list and silently returned to the player's inventory on respawn (overflow drops on the ground if full). Everything else drops normally.
- XP softened, not reset: vanilla resets level to 0 and spawns orbs worth up to min(level x 7, 100). This instead drops no orbs and subtracts a flat 3
  levels (floored at 0) - remaining progress toward the next level is kept, then reset to 0% within the new, reduced level.
- Slower despawn: items dropped from a player's death take twice as long to despawn - 12,000 ticks (10 min) instead of vanilla's 6,000 (5 min).
  Implemented by tagging each death drop and cancelling its first despawn; vanilla resets a cancelled item's age to 0, so it simply lives a second
  full cycle. Like any dropped item it only ages while its chunk is loaded, so 10 minutes is of loaded time, not wall clock.
  (Was silently broken until 2026-09-19: tagging hooked EntityDropItemEvent, which Paper never fires for death drops - nothing was ever tagged and
  every death drop vanished at vanilla's 5 minutes. Tagging now matches each spawning item against the death's own drop list.)

XP gain cap (XpCapListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- New mechanic: each player can gain at most 500 raw XP points (not levels) within any rolling 5-minute, server-wide-aligned window. Applies to any
  positive XP source routed through the vanilla point-grant event (mob kills, orb pickup, mining, breeding, fishing, furnace pickup, villager
  trading). "/xp add ... levels" and other direct level grants are a separate event this cap doesn't touch.

Sleep streaks (SleepStreakListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- New mechanic: sleeping through the night on consecutive nights builds a streak. Starting on the 2nd consecutive night, each additional night adds
  another 25% chance of a 20-minute thunderstorm rolling in right after everyone wakes (2nd = 25%, 3rd = 50%, 4th = 75%, 5th = guaranteed). A
  triggered storm - or a night that passes without a qualifying sleep - resets the streak to 0. (The step was 1/3 per night, guaranteed by night 4,
  until 2026-09-20.)
- The reset happens at sunrise on any unslept night, not lazily at the next sleep. (Until 2026-09-18 it was lazy: the stored streak was only ever
  touched by a sleep, so the storm odds were right but every announcement reported a stale, already-broken streak.)
- Straw beds (new in 26.3) are covered by all of this: they have no separate event, so they build the streak and are refused during a storm exactly
  like an ordinary bed.
- Entering a bed is also blocked outright during any thunderstorm (vanilla or plugin-triggered), so a player can't sleep through the punishment storm
  to keep the streak alive. Both the thunder and storm flags must be set, since the thunder flag alone can linger after a weather transition with a
  clear, bright sky - blocking sleep then looked like the plugin inventing a storm that wasn't there.
- Vanilla's daytime refusal message is reworded from "You can sleep only at night or during thunderstorms" to "You can sleep only at night", since
  this plugin makes the thunderstorm half untrue. Only that one message is replaced; other bed refusals are left alone.
- Chat/console announcements, to the affected world: the streak at each nightfall, an increase when a night is slept through, a reset when day breaks
  on an unslept night (only if there was a streak to lose), the storm rolling in, and that storm passing. A vanilla storm ending says nothing, because
  it didn't reset anything.
- Both streak announcements quote the storm odds that streak is buying, rather than leaving players to work them out: nightfall reports what sleeping
  tonight would risk ("Current sleep streak: 2. Sleeping tonight: 50% chance of a thunderstorm."), and a slept-through night reports what sleeping
  again tomorrow would. The tomorrow-night figure is omitted when that sleep just triggered a storm, since the streak is back to 0 and the storm
  message arrives a tick later anyway. Getting that right means the storm is rolled before the announcement is composed, not after it is sent.
- Day and night are read from the world clock (ticks 12523-23478, via the shared WorldClock helper), not sky brightness. Brightness is what Minecraft's own isDayTime() measures, and
  weather dims it: a daytime thunderstorm read as "night", and rain clearing at dusk read as a false sunrise that wiped a streak. Worlds with no
  day/night cycle (Nether, End) are skipped entirely.

Weapon/tool damage & attack speed (WeaponStatListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Base player attack damage (1.0) and attack speed (4.0) are vanilla; tool/weapon modifiers are stored under vanilla's own attribute IDs
(minecraft:base_attack_damage / minecraft:base_attack_speed) rather than a plugin-namespaced one, so tooltips render exactly like an unmodified
vanilla item's - green, unsigned, damage above speed, full computed total - instead of a generic blue/signed bonus line. Because a custom modifier
replaces vanilla's entire per-item default, every overridden item below restates both damage and speed even where only one actually changed:

  Item                Vanilla damage / speed        New damage / speed
  Copper Axe           9.0 / 0.8                      7.0 / 0.9
  Iron Axe             9.0 / 0.9                      7.0 / 1.0
  Iron Hoe             1.0 / 3.0                      1.0 / 2.0
  Stone Axe            9.0 / 0.8                      7.0 / 0.9

  Diamond Shovel       5.5 / 1.0                      6.5 / 1.0
  Diamond Spear        4.0 / 0.95                     5.0 / 0.95
  Netherite Sword      8.0 / 1.6                      9.0 / 1.6
  Netherite Pickaxe    6.0 / 1.2                      7.0 / 1.2
  Netherite Shovel     6.5 / 1.0                      7.5 / 1.0

Applied at crafting, on pickup, and re-verified against every item in a player's inventory on join - checked against the actual stored modifier

Unbreaking enchantment (UnbreakingListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Vanilla's damage-negation chance is 100 / (level + 1) for tools and weapons; this replaces it with a shallower curve, 100 / (1 + level / 3):

  Unbreaking level   Vanilla chance to still take damage   New chance
  I                  50%                                    75%
  II                 33.3%                                  60%
  III                25%                                    50%

Vanilla uses a second, shallower curve for armour - 1 - 2L/(5L+5), i.e. 80% / 73.3% / 70% - which the same replacement curve above now also applies
to, so armour is slightly better off at level I and worse off at level III than vanilla.

- Applies to every item Unbreaking can go on and every action that costs durability - bows, crossbows, tridents, shields, fishing rods, shears, flint
  & steel and brushes included, alongside the tools, weapons, armour and Elytra that were always covered.
- How, since 2026-09-20: by scaling the damage rather than by re-rolling the chance. Vanilla rolls Unbreaking internally and only raises its
  durability event for damage that survived - a roll of zero skips the event entirely - so a plugin cannot make damage land more often than vanilla
  decided, only change the number it is handed. Multiplying that number by (new curve / vanilla curve) puts the expected durability loss per use
  exactly on the curve in the table: x1.5 / x1.8 / x2.0 for tools and weapons, x0.9375 / x0.8182 / x0.7143 for armour. Unbreaking III on a tool
  therefore fires at vanilla's 25% and takes 2 points, costing the same over an item's life as the intended 50% chance of 1 point. Verified by
  simulation across tools, 2-point sword block breaks and multi-point armour hits.
- Trade-off: granularity, not rate. Wear arrives in fewer, larger steps than the percentages literally describe and any individual use can differ;
  total item lifetime cannot. Where the multiplier is below 1 (armour), a scaled roll can come out at zero points and the damage is dropped.
- Vanilla's own rules still apply and simply arrive as a number to scale: swords costing 2 durability per block broken, armour taking a fraction of
  the hit, an Elytra costing 1 per second airborne, and an item breaking at 0.
- Bug fixed 2026-09-20: before this, nothing outside a fixed list of four actions took durability damage at all. The previous design cancelled
  vanilla's durability event for every Unbreaking item and re-applied the loss by hand from block-break, melee, armour-damage and Elytra handlers, so
  anything no handler reached had its damage cancelled with nothing putting it back. An Unbreaking bow was simply indestructible, as were crossbows,
  tridents, shields, fishing rods, shears, flint & steel and brushes. Scaling the event instead removes the class of bug: there is no list of covered
  actions left to be missing from.

Elytra & Mending (ElytraEnchantListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Mending can no longer end up on an Elytra by any means - stripped if produced by combining at an anvil, and defensively stripped if it's ever
  somehow rolled at an enchanting table.

Anvil repair-with-material (AnvilRepairListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- For iron/gold/diamond/netherite tools, weapons, and armor: vanilla's "1 ingot restores 25% durability" is fully replaced. Ingots no longer repair
  these items - instead, 1 block of the matching tier material (Iron/Gold/Diamond/Netherite Block) restores 75% durability, consuming as many blocks
  as needed (up to what's available, so at most 2).
- Each block repair raises the item's prior-work penalty - the hidden counter added to every future anvil cost - by 50% more than a vanilla anvil use
  would. Vanilla takes n to 2n+1 (an increase of n+1); this takes n to n + ceil(1.5 x (n+1)). From new, that runs 0, 2, 7, 19, 49 against vanilla's
  0, 1, 3, 7, 15, 31, so an item reaches "Too Expensive!" after 4 block repairs instead of 6. The higher penalty carries into anything else done with
  that item at an anvil afterwards.
- Level cost follows vanilla's formula: the item's existing penalty plus 1 level per block consumed.
- Two long-standing bugs fixed alongside that (2026-09-19): block repairs never raised the penalty at all (the result was a straight copy of the
  input), and the level cost ignored the item's anvil history entirely, charging 1 level per block however worn the item was. Vanilla's "Too
  Expensive!" refusal at 40 levels was also being skipped, because it is checked before the plugin substitutes its own result; it is now re-applied
  (creative exempt).
- Known gap, unchanged: renaming an item in the same operation as a block repair still discards the new name.


4. CRAFTING & RECIPES (CustomRecipes, RecipeDiscoveryListener)
==================================================================
- Ingot -> block gear recipes: every iron, gold, and diamond tool, weapon, armor piece, and the Shield now use a block of the tier material instead of
  loose ingots/gems - 1 block per ingot slot for tools/weapons/shield, 2 blocks for armor pieces. Every valid slot arrangement is registered as its
  own recipe variant, so the block can go in any of the original ingot slots.
- Enchanting Table reworked the same way: both of its Diamonds become Diamond Blocks (the Book and 4 Obsidian are unchanged). It keeps vanilla's Misc
  recipe-book tab rather than the Equipment tab the gear recipes use.
- One recipe-book entry per item: only the first slot arrangement is ever shown. The others stay fully craftable but are never revealed, so a pickaxe
  occupies one book entry instead of one per block position. Of 255 registered recipes, 201 are hidden alternates, leaving 54 book entries. Minecraft
  draws one fixed shape per recipe and offers no way to cycle a single entry through several, so this consolidates the display rather than rotating
  it. Crafting via a hidden arrangement does not add it to the book.
- Recipes now appear in the book once a player is holding the ingredients, like vanilla's own. Vanilla drives that off per-recipe advancements that
  only exist for minecraft:-namespaced recipes, so the 237 recipes registered under this plugin's namespace were previously invisible until a player
  happened to craft one by hand - which is what made them show up "only after making one". Players carrying alternates unlocked by the older build get
  them cleared on join.
- Netherite Block recipe rebuilt as a fixed 2x2 shape (top-left of the grid) of Netherite Ingots.
- Netherite smithing upgrades switched to Block: every smithing-table upgrade recipe now takes a Netherite Block as its "addition" instead of an
  ingot.
- Chain output doubled (vanilla's ingredients/shape otherwise untouched).
- Chainmail armor recipes added: no vanilla recipe exists for any of the 4 pieces; this adds one for each, mirroring other armor tiers' shapes with
  Chains filling every ingredient slot.
- Leather armor reworked: all 4 recipes replaced - instead of leather-only, each needs Leather (or Rotten Flesh, 1-for-1) plus String and Wood Planks
  (any type), using the same L/S/P shapes as other armor tiers.
- Recipe book category fixed: every rebuilt recipe above is explicitly placed in the Equipment tab. A freshly constructed recipe defaults to Misc, and
  since each fully replaces the vanilla recipe under its original key, leaving the default would have silently moved them all to Misc.


5. VILLAGER TRADING - LIBRARIANS (VillagerTradeListener)
==================================================================
Every librarian trade for levels 1-4 (Novice-Expert) is generated directly by this listener - no bundled datapack. Level 5 ("Master") adds nothing: a
librarian just keeps whatever book it already rolled at level 3. Vanilla's own trade-unlock pipeline still fires as a librarian levels up, but any
enchanted-book trade it tries to add is cancelled outright at every level (see safety net below); the listener tops up whatever's missing for the
villager's current level each time a player opens its trade menu.

- Novice (1): always has both "24 Paper -> 1 Emerald" (16 uses) and "9 Emerald -> 1 Bookshelf" (12 uses) - matching vanilla exactly, with vanilla's
  own generic enchanted-book candidate (normally also in this pool) kept out.
- Apprentice (2): always has both "4 Book -> 1 Emerald" (12 uses) and "1 Emerald -> 1 Lantern" (12 uses).
- Journeyman (3):
    - Always exactly one of "5 Ink Sac -> 1 Emerald" or "1 Emerald -> 4 Glass" (12 uses), chosen once per villager and fixed for life.
    - Always guaranteed an enchanted-book trade, chosen once and fixed for life (reaching level 5 doesn't re-roll it). With trade_rebalance enabled,
      the enchant is drawn with even odds from a biome-specific pool: that biome's 2-3 regular enchants (level 1-2, 5-32 emeralds) plus its
      strongest/"Master"-tier enchant (table below) as one more equally-likely option, at its fixed level and vanilla's higher master-tier price
      (11-36 emeralds at level 3, 8-26 lower). With trade_rebalance disabled, the enchant is instead drawn uniformly from the full generic pool
      (regular-tier only, no biome flavor) - same as vanilla's non-biome-specific trades.
- Expert (4):
    - Always guaranteed a "2 Writable Books -> 1 Emerald" trade.
    - Never offers a plain enchanted-book trade.
    - Always exactly one of "5 Emerald -> 1 Clock" or "4 Emerald -> 1 Compass" (12 uses), chosen once and fixed thereafter.
- Each biome's folded-in strongest enchant is checked directly against this Minecraft version's actual trade_rebalance data (extracted from the server
  jar). Swamp is changed from vanilla's Mending to Thorns II; the other six match vanilla exactly:

    Biome        Strongest enchantment
    Desert       Efficiency III
    Jungle       Unbreaking II
    Plains       Protection III
    Savanna      Sharpness III
    Snow         Silk Touch I
    Swamp        Thorns II  (vanilla: Mending)
    Taiga        Fortune II

- Safety net, all levels: any enchanted-book trade vanilla's own trade-unlock pipeline tries to add (base pool, or the trade_rebalance overlay if
  enabled) is cancelled outright - every enchanted-book trade a librarian ends up with comes from this listener alone (this is what fixed the original
  "enchanted book at Novice level" bug).
- Curses excluded: Curse of Binding and Curse of Vanishing are in no pool, and any curse-enchanted book a librarian already has (from before this
  rule, or another source) is stripped and replaced the next time its trade menu opens. Frost Walker stays tradeable (Snow's biome pool, plus the
  generic fallback) - it's a treasure enchantment, not a curse. Mending is never in either pool.


6. FARMING & FOOD
==================================================================

Crop growth (CropGrowthListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Applies to everything in vanilla's own #minecraft:crops tag - Wheat, Carrots, Potatoes, Beetroot, Pumpkin/Melon stems, and (added 2026-09-19)
Torchflower and Pitcher crops, which an older hand-kept list had missed. Nether Wart is not in that tag and stays excluded, as do plants that don't
grow on farmland (sugar cane, cactus, bamboo, berries, cocoa, kelp):
- Half growth rate at night and during thunderstorms - vanilla grows on random ticks regardless of time or weather. This was a total block until
  2026-09-18; halving it instead cut the time for a full field by ~29%.
- Night is taken from the world clock (ticks 12523-23478, via the shared WorldClock helper), not sky brightness, so ordinary rain around dusk no
  longer counts as night. Thunderstorms
  are penalised explicitly instead.
- Requires a clear sky (sky light 15 in the column above) - glass, trapdoors, and bars still allow growth, but any solid roof blocks it entirely.
  Vanilla farmland crops don't need sky access at all.
- No crop may run more than 2 growth stages ahead of the least-grown crop of the same type within 4 blocks; one sitting at that trailing edge grows at
  full rate. This is what keeps a field maturing together: vanilla growth is a per-block lottery, so a field planted in one go finishes raggedly.
  Simulated over a solid 9x9 hydrated wheat plot with the chunk held loaded, the gap between the first and last crop maturing falls from ~88 min to
  ~46 min, with the full field taking ~186 min against vanilla's ~103. The cost is that nothing ripens early any more: the first crop now lands at
  ~137 min instead of ~15.
- Consequence worth knowing: one crop replanted into an otherwise mature field counts as the trailing edge and briefly holds the rest back. It
  resolves on its own, since trailing crops grow at full rate.
- The old "2/3 of growth rolls kept" thinning is retired (set to 1.0). Once the neighbour cap existed it was worth only ~10% of the field's time,
  because trailing crops skip the thinning roll and most crops sit at the trailing edge most of the time.
- Bone meal is entirely exempt and behaves exactly as in vanilla - no sky requirement, no night penalty, and it can push a crop past its neighbours.
  (Until 2026-09-19 every rule above applied to bone meal too, since Paper reports bone-meal growth through the same event as natural growth; the
  neighbour cap refused it outright on any crop already 2 stages ahead.)
- Unaffected: a stem's Pumpkin or Melon still appears at vanilla's rate once the stem is mature, because that growth is reported against the empty
  block the fruit fills, not the stem.

Animal breeding growth (BreedingGrowthListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Baby animals produced by breeding (not wild-spawned) take 3x as long to mature - their starting negative age counter is multiplied by 3 at birth.
  Vanilla's ~20-minute maturation becomes ~60 minutes under this plugin.

Food saturation (FoodSaturationListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Doubles the saturation value (satiety duration, not nutrition/hunger restored) of the 5 breedable-animal meats (raw and cooked) plus plain
farmland-crop foods and bread:

  Food              Vanilla saturation   New saturation
  Beef (raw)        1.8                   3.6
  Cooked Beef       12.8                  25.6
  Porkchop (raw)    1.8                   3.6
  Cooked Porkchop   12.8                  25.6
  Chicken (raw)     1.2                   2.4
  Cooked Chicken    7.2                   14.4
  Mutton (raw)      1.2                   2.4
  Cooked Mutton     9.6                   19.2
  Rabbit (raw)      1.8                   3.6
  Cooked Rabbit     6.0                   12.0
  Carrot            3.6                   7.2
  Potato            0.6                   1.2
  Baked Potato      6.0                   12.0
  Beetroot          1.2                   2.4
  Melon Slice       1.2                   2.4
  Bread             6.0                   12.0

Fish, rotten flesh, composite dishes (pumpkin pie, beetroot soup, stews), and non-farmland foods (apples, berries, golden carrot) are unaffected.


7. COSMETIC / NON-MECHANICAL ADDITION
==================================================================

Custom Enderman dodge sound (EndermanDodgeSoundPackListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Hosts a tiny resource pack (a self-run local HTTP server) that adds one new, purely additive sound event, played when an Enderman's dodge-teleport
lands with the "behind the player" bias. It never overrides any vanilla sound and layers safely alongside a player's own resource packs.
- Host and port come from config.yml (resource-pack-host, resource-pack-port; defaults 127.0.0.1 and 25566). The default only reaches players
  connecting from the same machine the server runs on - a public deployment needs a reachable host set here and that port opened.
- Pack format declares 97 (26.3) and accepts formats 88 upwards. It previously pinned max_format to 88 (26.2 only), which made 26.3 clients reject the
  whole pack: every vanilla Enderman sound kept working while this one silently never played.


8. WORLD / DIMENSIONS
==================================================================

Nether portal scaling (NetherPortalScaleListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Vanilla links the Nether and Overworld at an 8:1 ratio (1 Nether block = 8 Overworld blocks); this is rewritten to a flat 1:1 ratio. The X/Z an
  entity stands on when stepping through a portal is the X/Z it arrives at on the other side, unscaled, still clamped to the destination world's
  border. Target world, Y, and facing are left untouched - only X/Z scaling changes.
- End portals are unaffected - the End's coordinate_scale already matches the Overworld's in vanilla.

Nether roof void damage (NetherRoofVoidDamageListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- New mechanic: the Nether's bedrock roof can never generate above Y=127 (derived from the dimension's noise settings - min_y 0, height 128, roof
  carved 6 blocks from the top, matching logical_height of 128). Any player at Y >= 128 while in the Nether takes vanilla's out-of-world damage (4
  dmg/10 ticks), bypassing Creative/Spectator invulnerability and totems, exactly like vanilla's own below-world void damage.


9. LOOT TABLES
==================================================================

Fishing treasure pool (FishingLootListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Vanilla's fishing treasure pool has 6 equal-weight entries (name tag, saddle, bow, fishing rod, book, nautilus shell), making an enchanted book a
  1-in-6 (~16.7%) pick. This halves it to 1-in-12 (~8.3%): on a book catch, a coin flip either keeps it or re-rolls the real vanilla treasure table
  (rejecting further books), so the freed-up probability lands back on the other five entries in their original proportions instead of being deleted.

Fortune on ore blocks (FortuneLootListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Applies to coal, copper, diamond, emerald, gold, iron, lapis, nether gold, and nether quartz ore (+ deepslate variants), and amethyst clusters -
  every block using vanilla's minecraft:ore_drops Fortune formula. Blocks using a different bonus formula (redstone, glowstone, etc.) are untouched
  here - the crops among them are covered by CropFortuneListener below - and Silk Touch bypasses this entirely, same as vanilla.

    Fortune level   Vanilla (~uniform)              New probability table
    I               1x 66.7%, 2x 33.3%              1x 60%, 2x 40%
    II              1x 50%, 2x 25%, 3x 25%           1x 40%, 2x 50%, 3x 10%
    III             1x 40%, 2x 20%, 3x 20%, 4x 20%   1x 20%, 2x 60%, 3x 10%,
                                                      4x 10%

  Fortune levels above III (only reachable via commands) fall through to unmodified vanilla behavior.
- Lapis Ore's base drop (no Fortune) is flattened from vanilla's randomized 4-9 to a flat 1, matching every other ore in this list - Fortune scales
  the count from there using the table above, not vanilla's randomized base roll.

Fortune on crops (CropFortuneListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Fortune no longer raises crop yields at all. Breaking a mature crop with a Fortune tool drops exactly what the same break would have dropped with an
  unenchanted one, so farming scales by planting more rather than by enchanting the hoe.
- Covers the crops vanilla actually scales with Fortune: wheat and beetroot (their seeds), carrots and potatoes (the vegetable itself), nether wart,
  and melon blocks. Crops Fortune never affected (cocoa, sweet berries, pumpkin, torchflower, pitcher pod) need nothing and are not listed.
- The replacement yield is re-rolled from the broken block's own loot table, via BlockState#getDrops with a copy of the tool that has Fortune stripped
  off - there is no drop table written out here, so nothing to drift when a crop is retuned upstream, and the parts Fortune never touched (a potato's
  2% poisonous potato, for instance) keep their vanilla odds.
- Because both rolls are random, the plain re-roll can come out above what Fortune happened to produce - a Fortune I hoe draws zero wheat seeds about
  3% of the time, where the plain roll usually would not. Clamping downward only would quietly leave crops worse than vanilla, so a shortfall is
  dropped directly at the block instead.

End City treasure chests (EndCityLootListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Loot weight changes: applied post-hoc to vanilla's own generated loot - each roll's real item is kept, dropped, or duplicated with probability
  derived from newWeight/oldWeight, so untouched entries keep their exact original odds instead of being renormalized against a shared pool total.

    Entry                           Vanilla weight   New weight
    Diamond gear (8 pieces, each)   3                1
    Gold ingot                      15               20
    Iron ingot                      10               15
    Diamond                         5                7
    Emerald                         2                3

  Everything else (iron gear, saddle, horse armor, beetroot seeds, the smithing template pool) is unchanged.
- Diamond gear enchantment pool restricted: vanilla enchants the diamond (and iron) gear here from #minecraft:on_random_loot (levels 20-39), which
  includes 4 treasure enchantments a normal enchanting table could never roll - Mending, Frost Walker, and both curses. For diamond gear only, any
  treasure enchantment vanilla picked is discarded and re-rolled using only non-treasure enchantments (#minecraft:in_enchanting_table), reading each
  candidate's real weight/cost-range/item-compatibility live from the server's enchantment registry rather than a hardcoded list. Iron gear is
  unaffected and can still roll Mending, Frost Walker, or either curse.

Concrete powder in water (ConcretePowderListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- New mechanic: vanilla only converts concrete powder to concrete when it's a placed block touching water; the dropped/thrown item form never
  converts. A concrete powder item stack (any amount) sitting continuously in water for 3 seconds is swapped in place for the matching concrete color,
  same stack size. Leaving the water resets the wait.

Pet health display (PetHealthDisplayListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- New mechanic: any tamed pet (Wolf, Cat, Parrot, or a tamed horse/donkey/mule/llama - anything Tameable once owned) shows current/max health as an
  always-visible floating label above its name-tag height. This is a separate label (an invisible marker armor stand), not the pet's own custom name,
  so a real name tag and vanilla's default death message stay untouched - an earlier version wrote health straight into the pet's customName,
  polluting real names and death messages; any pet still carrying that pollution gets repaired to its real name (or cleared) on sight. Repositioned
  every tick to track a moving pet continuously; the health text is only re-sent when it actually changes. Removed the moment its pet dies.

Cat nine lives (CatLivesListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- New mechanic: cats have double vanilla's max health (10 -> 20) and nine lives. A blow that would kill a cat instead spends a life - the damage is
  cancelled, the cat is restored to full health, and the Totem of Undying sound plays. The ninth such blow is the one that sticks: a cat survives
  eight deaths and dies on the next, and the counter reads 9 down to 1 before the cat is gone.
- The count shows on its own gold floating label ("9 lives", "1 life") above the cat, 0.3 blocks above where the health label sits so the two stack
  rather than overlap. Always visible, on stray cats as well as tamed ones - deliberately independent of the health label, which is permanent once a
  cat is tamed but only look-triggered while it is stray.
- Lives live in the cat's own persistent data, not a plugin-side map, so the count survives restarts, chunk unloads and being carried between worlds.
  Cats that predate this listener start with the full nine the first time they are seen, and the doubled health is applied as a keyed attribute
  modifier so the same sweep can't double it twice.
- A revive also extinguishes the cat and grants the same brief invulnerability window an ordinary hit does. Cancelling the damage event means vanilla
  never sets that window itself, so without both a cat could burn or be hit through several lives in the space of a second.
- Exempt, and fatal however many lives remain: /kill, the void, and the world border (reviving in the last two just feeds the cat back into the same
  damage next tick). Anything that kills without raising a damage event at all - a plugin calling setHealth(0) - can't be intercepted either.

Hostile mob and passive animal health display (MobHealthDisplayListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- New mechanic: any hostile mob (Zombie, Skeleton, Creeper, Spider, Enderman, Slime, Ghast, Shulker, etc. - anything Enemy) or untamed passive animal
  (cows, sheep, pigs, chickens, wild horses - anything Animals) shows current/max health
  the same way as the pet display above, except the label only exists while a player is actually looking directly at that mob - appearing and
  disappearing with their aim rather than staying up permanently. "Looking at" requires both a precise crosshair hit on the mob's hitbox and an
  unobstructed line of sight (can't be seen through a wall the crosshair ray geometrically passes behind). Polled every tick for both the aim check
  and label position.
- Tamed animals are excluded here, since the pet display above already gives them a permanent label; without that exclusion a pet would carry two
  stacked labels. Look-triggered rather than always-visible matters more for animals than it did for hostiles: a render distance routinely holds whole
  herds and full farm pens, and permanent labels would mean an armor stand per animal, all re-positioned every tick.

Tamed wolves (TamedWolfListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Max health raised from vanilla's 40 to 100, and melee damage increased 50% above vanilla's base. Applied on taming (deferred a tick so it lands
  after vanilla's own post-tame processing, since applying it same-tick was observed getting silently reverted), and reinforced by a periodic scan
  over every tamed wolf in the world - covering puppies bred from two already-tamed wolves (pre-tamed, no taming event to hook) and wolves already
  tamed before this listener loaded (e.g. an earlier session). Max health is corrected on every scan pass regardless of prior state; only the
  attack-damage multiply (unsafe to repeat) is gated behind a persistent "already buffed" tag. Wild, untamed wolves are unaffected.

  one ships). plugin.yml declares api-version 26.3, so the plugin will not load on an older server. Requires Java 25, as Paper 26.x does.
- Crop-growth timings quoted above come from a Monte-Carlo simulation calibrated to vanilla's random-tick rate (3/4096 per block per tick) and its 1/6
  growth roll for a solid field, with the chunk held loaded throughout - not from measured in-game play.
- Vanilla comparison numbers above use long-stable Minecraft values (durability, saturation, attack damage/speed, follow
  range, explosion radius, step height, jump strength, the Unbreaking formula, and the ~1-in-100,000 lightning roll) that have held constant across
  many versions.
- The strongest enchant folded into each of the seven biomes' Journeyman pools is verified directly against this Minecraft version's actual
  trade_rebalance data (extracted from the server jar). Swamp is the only intentional deviation (Mending -> Thorns II); the other six match vanilla's
  own values exactly.
- Likewise, the fishing/Fortune/End City vanilla baselines in section 9, and the Y=127 nether roof ceiling in section 8, are all read directly from
  this version's actual loot table and noise settings data (extracted from the server jar), not guessed.
