VANILLAKFC - FULL LIST OF GAMEPLAY CHANGES FROM VANILLA MINECRAFT
==================================================================

Complete inventory of every gameplay change made by VanillaKFC, derived directly from the source (src/main/java/com/example/vanillakfc/**,
src/main/resources/resourcepack/**). It's a Paper server plugin (targets Paper API 26.3), not a client mod - it only affects servers running this
jar.

Where vanilla has a well-established, stable value, it's listed for comparison.

1. COMBAT & HOSTILE MOBS
==================================================================

Creepers (CreeperListener, CreeperBehaviorTask)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Max health 20->12 (6 hearts). Was 16 from 2026-10-07, lowered to 12 on 2026-10-09. Applied as creepers spawn, like the rest of this list, so
  creepers already in the world keep what they spawned with.
- Perception & mobility: follow range 16->32 blocks; step height 0.6->1.2 (clears a full block without jumping); jump strength x1.5.
- Explosion radius: 3->4 (6->8 charged); vanilla's charged-doubling still applies on top.
- Natural spawning: only 35% of spawn attempts succeed (vanilla: 100%, solo). A successful spawn can add a 2nd creeper within 24 blocks (50% chance,
  capped at 2 total).
- Active chase: while targeting a player (and not frozen, see below), re-paths straight at them at speed factor 2.5 (was 3.0 until 2026-09-24; at
  3.0 a chase peaked near 1.2 blocks/tick, about four times sprinting, which turned every small detour around a block edge into a large, fast swerve). Throttled to once per 0.75 blocks
  of target movement or every 3 ticks, rather than every tick, to stop erratic path flip-flopping around obstacles.
- Chase smoothness fixed 2026-09-24: that throttle used to be 2 blocks / 10 ticks, which at speed factor 3.0 (~0.57 blocks per tick) let a creeper
  outrun its own path - it reached the end of a route aimed at where the player had been, then spent the rest of the gap with no path at all,
  coasting on momentum. Measured over a straight 30-block pursuit of a walking target: 57% of ticks with no live path, average heading 27-47 degrees
  off the target, and ~20% of ticks moving away from it - the "jittering off in odd directions" this was reported as. At 3 ticks / 0.75 blocks the
  same chase holds 14-18 degrees, never moves away from the target, and closes 20% sooner (85 vs 108 ticks). Re-measured on an obstacle course of
  walls and pillars to confirm the flip-flopping the throttle guards against did not return: direction reversals went 3 -> 2, contact 194 -> 171
  ticks. Vanilla's own goals were ruled out as the cause - the random-stroll goal never took the navigation over in any run, and removing it changed
  nothing.
- Target re-acquisition (2026-09-24): the instant a creeper stops being watched it re-targets the nearest player it has line of sight to, instead of
  waiting for vanilla's own targeting goal to come round again. Freezing works by stripping the target every tick, so every glance at a creeper left
  it with nothing to chase when the player turned away, and vanilla's random-stroll goal filled the gap. Measured over a real chase (run, turn,
  watch, repeat): the creeper was targetless for 48% of its unfrozen ticks with the player 5-32 blocks away, crawling at 0.06 blocks/tick and walking
  away from them on 43% of ticks. After the fix, on the same test: 0% targetless, 3% moving away, average heading error 75 -> 26 degrees, and average
  chase speed 0.39 -> 0.76 blocks/tick. Line of sight is still required, so it only restores the target vanilla would have picked anyway.
- Contact hand-off (2026-09-24): inside 2 blocks of its target the chase stops steering entirely and leaves the creeper to vanilla's own melee/swell
  goals. At speed factor 3.0 it covers most of that distance in a single tick, so it would overshoot whatever point it was sent to and circle its
  target correcting back and forth; measured, handing over roughly halves that close-range thrashing (average heading error while in contact 67 -> 43
  degrees). The throttle state is cleared on hand-off, so the instant the player opens the gap again the chase re-paths on the next tick.
- Short-term memory: after losing sight of its target, keeps walking toward its last known position for 3s if that spot is still within 16 blocks.
  Movement only - doesn't restore the actual target, so swelling/igniting still needs a genuine re-spot.
- Freeze-when-watched: while any player has unobstructed line of sight (40 blocks, ~90 degree cone, 9 hitbox sample points), movement speed is zeroed
  (can still turn to face them). "Any player" is literal and always has been - a bystander watching a creeper chase someone else freezes it just as
  dead as the person it is after. The creeper's own target has no special standing in the check.
- See-through blocks don't count as obstruction (as of 2026-09-20). The sight ray stops at anything with a collision box, so a player watching from
  inside a base - through a window, over a fence, behind iron bars - was not registering as watching at all, and the creeper carried on as if
  unobserved. Glass (all colours, tinted included), glass panes, iron bars, chains, fences and fence gates, scaffolding, ice, barriers and light
  blocks are now transparent to the check. Slabs, stairs and walls still block it - they really do hide what's behind them.
- Fixed 2026-10-07 - creepers lunging at a player who was looking at them: a creeper only ever moves while the check says nobody is watching (a
  frozen one's speed is multiplied to exactly zero), so any lunge under a player's gaze meant the check thought it was hidden. Leaves were the main
  cause - a creeper behind a bush or at the edge of a tree is in plain view through the gaps, but counted as unseen, so after the 4-tick grace it
  went straight into the 2.5x chase. A sight line now counts as clear through up to 2 leaf blocks (one deep inside a tree still hides it). Copper
  grates (all variants), spawners, trial spawners, vaults, mangrove roots, and slime and honey blocks are now see-through as well.
- Freezing stops the creeper outright, not just its steering (as of 2026-09-20). Zeroing the speed attribute leaves two things running: the momentum
  of a chase at speed factor 3.0, which friction takes the better part of a second to bleed off while the creeper keeps drifting toward its target,
  and the queued path, ready to resume the instant the freeze lifts. Both are now cleared on the tick the freeze goes on. Horizontal velocity is
  zeroed only on that transition, not every frozen tick, so a watched creeper can still be knocked back when hit.
- Fuse blocked while watched: can't start swelling (hit-trigger or proximity/LoS trigger) while watched, unless already mid-fuse - a fuse that
  started while unwatched keeps climbing and detonates on schedule even if someone then looks at it.
- Fixed 2026-10-07 - creepers fusing while being looked at: that mid-fuse exception was only withdrawn while the creeper was unwatched. A creeper
  whose fuse started unwatched and then wound back down while someone was watching it (they backed off out of range) kept the exception for good,
  kept its target, and fused and exploded again as soon as that player walked back up to it, looking right at it. It now ends the moment the fuse
  stops climbing, watched or not, so the next approach is stopped like any other.
- Flees cats within 12 blocks - unchanged vanilla behavior, just replicated (no exposed API to retune it).
- Charged creepers are exempt from all of the above - always treated as "unwatched."

Happy Ghasts (HappyGhastListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- A harnessed Happy Ghast has 100 max health (50 hearts) instead of vanilla's 20, so something you fly around on can take a hit instead of dying to a
  stray skeleton on the way up.
- "Harnessed" is the definition of a pet here, because a Happy Ghast has no owner the way a wolf or cat does - it isn't Tameable at all - and the
  harness is the only in-game mark of one being claimed rather than wild. It is what vanilla itself checks (isWearingBodyArmor) before allowing a
  rider. Wild Happy Ghasts and ghastlings keep vanilla's 20.
- The boost follows the harness rather than being granted once: removing the harness drops the ceiling back to 20 and brings any health above it down
  too, so a harness can't be fitted and immediately taken off to keep the health for free. Fitting one on a ghast that was at full health tops it up
  to the new 100; a wounded one stays wounded.
- Applied as a keyed attribute modifier derived from the base value rather than a hard-coded +80, so the total is exactly 100 whatever vanilla's base
  happens to be. Driven by a twice-a-second sweep rather than an equip event, since a harness can also arrive from a dispenser, a command, or a chunk
  loading a ghast that was already wearing one.
- A harnessed Happy Ghast also flies at 6.0 blocks/sec when ridden, against vanilla's ~3.29 - which is slower than walking (4.32) and well under
  sprinting (5.61). 6.0 puts it just above sprinting. Still nothing like an elytra (~30+); the point of a ghast is hovering and carrying, not speed.
- Done by raising the ghast's flying_speed attribute from vanilla's 0.05 to ~0.0676, on the same harness-gated, keyed-modifier basis as the health.
  Not a straight 1.83x, because vanilla's flying speed enters ridden movement twice - once scaling the rider's input (x3.9) and once as the flight
  speed itself (x5/3) - so terminal speed goes as its square, and x1.35 on the attribute is what doubles-ish the speed. The listener derives the
  attribute from the target blocks/sec and vanilla's own constants (including the 0.91-per-tick air drag) rather than hard-coding 0.0676, so the
  intent stays readable if any of them are retuned.
- Wild Happy Ghasts and ghastlings keep vanilla's speed, so nothing about the ones drifting around the Nether changes.

Endermen (EndermanListener, EndermanDodgeSoundPackListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Can no longer pick up blocks (placement unaffected).
- Melee damage is vanilla's again (Easy 4.5, Normal 7, Hard 10.5) as of 2026-10-11. It had a flat -2.0 taken off before that (2.5 / 5 / 8.5).
- Dodge: 50% chance to dodge any player melee hit entirely - teleports 3-7 blocks away, 65% biased into a 30 degree cone directly behind the attacker
  (custom sound on the biased case, vanilla teleport sound otherwise).
- Pillar-punish: a hit landed while the attacker is standing at least one block above the Enderman's feet has a 50% chance to trigger a counter
  instead - teleport directly behind the player, hit with knockback strong enough to knock them off their perch, teleport back ~0.4s later.
- Aggro teleport (added 2026-10-11): the moment an Enderman turns hostile toward a player - stared at, hit, or angered by a broken End Crystal
  (see the dragon fight) - it teleports to a random spot 2-5 blocks from that player, on solid footing, facing them, and plays a custom sound
  (vanillakfc:enderman_aggro, from uinote.mp3). The sound is played from the Enderman itself after the teleport, so it starts at the landing spot
  next to the player. Portal particles at both ends; no vanilla teleport sound.
    - Triggers on the Enderman taking the player as its target, whatever the cause, a tick after it happens. Once per Enderman per 5 seconds, so
      a target that flickers off and on doesn't cause repeat arrivals; one already after that player doesn't re-trigger.
    - With nowhere valid to land within 5 blocks (16 tries), it stays put and the sound still plays from where it is.
    - A dodge now counts as turning hostile (changed 2026-10-11): a dodged hit is cancelled, so vanilla never set the Enderman on its attacker
      and it would dodge and then just stand there. The dodge now sets it on the attacker itself (Survival/Adventure players only).
    - The sound always plays on a teleport, but not always this one's. If a dodge or a pillar-punish has already teleported the Enderman when it
      turns hostile, that teleport stands - a second jump would undo the dodge, and the pillar-punish is about to send it back anyway - and the
      aggro sound plays from where it put the Enderman. On a "behind the player" dodge that means both custom sounds play together.
    - Vanilla still applies afterwards: an Enderman being stared at from under 4 blocks teleports away again on its own.
    - Players who declined the resource pack see the teleport but hear nothing.

Skeletons (SkeletonListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Movement speed cut to x0.2 while actively targeting (stationary snipers instead of walking shooters).
- Follow range 16->32 blocks.
- Range limit (added 2026-10-07): a skeleton only keeps a target within 32 blocks and within 10 blocks of its own height, and drops it otherwise -
  checked every 2 ticks, well inside the 1-second bow draw, so an out-of-range shot never fires. Vanilla only checks range when a skeleton first
  finds a target on its own; a target picked up any other way - being hit, or this plugin's own fallback below - was kept at any distance, and at
  4x arrow speed a skeleton could hit a player on a Happy Ghast 130+ blocks overhead. The fallback below obeys the same limit.
- Shot interval hard-locked to an exact tick count: 120 ticks (6s) on Easy/Normal, 100 ticks (5s) on Hard - vs vanilla's ~60/~40-tick cooldowns -
  tracked by server tick count so it can't drift under lag. Every draw that starts always ends in a fired arrow.
- Every arrow leaves at 4x vanilla's velocity, re-aimed straight at the target instead of vanilla's slow-speed arc (which, scaled up as-is, would sail
  high over the target's head).
- Never idle with a target in view: since frozen movement stops it from repositioning to keep sight, a lost target now falls back to whoever last
  damaged it (if visible), then the nearest visible player - only goes target-less if neither is actually in view.
- Damage flattened to a fixed 3 (Easy) / 4 (Normal) / 5 (Hard), for both arrows and melee, replacing vanilla's velocity-dependent damage (which the 4x
  shot speed would otherwise inflate well past normal).

Spiders (SpiderListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Movement speed x1.5 (vanilla base 0.3). Cave Spiders are a distinct entity type and untouched by this listener.

Iron Golems (IronGolemListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Drop nothing on death - vanilla's iron ingots (0-2, more with Looting), poppy chance, and any XP are all cleared.

Zombies (ZombiePodListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Every natural zombie spawn additionally spawns one extra zombie at the same spot (tagged so it isn't doubled again), roughly doubling natural pack
  density.

Drowned (DrownedTridentListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Tridents thrown by a Drowned deal half damage (8 -> 4 raw, before armor). Only the thrown projectile is affected, matched by its shooter - a
  Drowned's melee hit is untouched, and so is any trident thrown or swung by a player. Halving happens pre-armor, so an armoured target takes slightly
  more than half of what it used to (armor absorbs a smaller share of a weaker hit); unarmoured, it's exactly half.

All hostile mobs - pod spawn cooldown (PodSpawnCooldownListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- New mechanic: once any hostile mob naturally spawns, no hostile mob of any species may naturally spawn within 6 blocks of that X/Z column (any
  height) for the next 5 minutes. Spawner spawns, breeding, and spawn eggs are unaffected - only natural world spawns are gated. Scoped to Bukkit's
  broad Enemy interface (includes Slime, MagmaCube, Ghast, Shulker, Phantom, Hoglin), not just the narrower Monster interface.

Mace (MaceDamageListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Raw base damage (hit + crit + fall-distance smash bonus, before armor/resistance) is capped at 40.0. Vanilla's fall-scaling smash bonus is otherwise
  unbounded, so extreme fall setups (elytra + wind charges, etc.) could produce arbitrarily large hits.

Shield break on heavy hits (ShieldBreakListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Added 2026-10-07.
- A hit worth more than 12 damage, blocked with a shield, knocks the shield down the way an axe does: the hit is still blocked, then the shield goes
  on a 5-second cooldown (an axe's) with vanilla's shield-break sound. Works on any blocked hit - melee, projectiles and explosions - from mobs
  and players alike.
- "Worth" is the hit before the shield took any of it, including difficulty scaling; 12 or less, nothing changes. For scale: a Hard-difficulty
  Iron Golem, Ravager or Warden hit, a close creeper blast, or a charged player hit with a strong weapon clears it, while ordinary zombie or
  skeleton hits don't.

Spear (SpearDamageListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- The charge-attack bonus (extra damage scaled by relative velocity between attacker and target) is capped at 5x whatever the hit would have dealt
  without it (base weapon damage, including Sharpness). Applies to all 7 tiers (Wood/Stone/Copper/Iron/Gold/Diamond/Netherite). Smite and Bane of
  the Living aren't factored into the reference damage the cap is based on, so a Smite/Bane spear's effective cap against its favored targets is
  slightly tighter than fully vanilla-accurate. Since Bane of the Living now boosts nearly every mob, that applies to most of a Bane spear's hits.

Beds & Respawn Anchors (BedExplosionListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Wrong-dimension explosion power (beds in the Nether/End, respawn anchors outside the Nether) is reduced from vanilla's 5.0 down to 1.0 - the same
  power as a ghast fireball.

Ender Dragon fight (DragonFightListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Added 2026-10-06.
- Crystals come back: every 30 seconds while a dragon is alive, if any obsidian pillar's End Crystal is gone, one empty pillar gets a new crystal
  on its bedrock - chosen at random if several are empty, so it's one per 30 seconds however many are missing. Caged pillars included. The dragon
  heals from it like any other crystal, so a fight needs to be won between respawns or with the crystals kept down.
- The ten pillars are found where vanilla generates them (evenly spaced on a circle of radius 42 around the island centre), and each one's top by
  its bedrock cap on obsidian - so a pillar whose bedrock is gone stops getting crystals. Pillars in unloaded chunks are skipped, not loaded.
- Nothing respawns once the dragon is dead, or during the respawn ritual.
- Breaking a crystal angers an enderman: when a player destroys any End Crystal by hitting it or with their own projectile (arrow, trident, etc.),
  one random enderman within 64 blocks of that player turns hostile toward that player only - the same anger as being stared at. One already
  attacking that player is skipped in favour of another. No enderman in range, no effect. Crystals destroyed by explosions or anything other than
  a player don't count.
- Arrival sound (added 2026-10-11): a player who loads into the End while a dragon is alive there hears a custom 7.7-second clip
  (vanillakfc:end_dragon_alive, from warthog.m4a), played to that player only, at full volume wherever they stand. "Loads in" means arriving
  from another dimension, or logging in already in the End. Nothing plays if the dragon is dead or mid-respawn.
    - The dragon isn't always loaded the instant a player arrives (and on a first-ever visit hasn't been created yet), so it's looked for
      every half second for the first 5 seconds and the clip plays the moment one is found.
    - On a login the clip waits for the resource pack to finish loading, since the sound is in the pack and the pack is only sent on join.
    - Players who declined the resource pack hear nothing.

Lightning (LightningListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Adds a second, independent ~1-in-100,000-per-loaded-chunk-per-tick strike roll during thunderstorms, striking the highest block in a random column
  of that chunk - vanilla's own roll still fires alongside it, combining into roughly double vanilla's normal strike rate.
- Lightning rods: like vanilla's own strikes, this roll first looks for the nearest lightning rod within 128 blocks and strikes the block above it
  instead. Bug fixed 2026-09-28: it used to strike the random column directly, so rods never caught any of the plugin's strikes - half of all storm
  lightning ignored rods. A rod only counts if nothing is on top of it (it has to be the highest block in its column), same as vanilla.
- Gated on both isThundering() and hasStorm() being true (not just the former) - separate flags that always agree under vanilla's natural cycle but
  could desync via a command or another plugin; requiring both closes off any way this roll could fire without an actual storm overhead.


2. DEATH, PROGRESSION & SLEEP
==================================================================

Death drops & XP loss (DeathDropListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Gear survives death: any sword, spear, pickaxe, axe, hoe, shovel, armor piece, shield, bow, crossbow, trident, elytra, mace, fishing rod,
  spyglass, shears, flint and steel, brush, carrot on a stick, warped fungus on a stick, or arrow (plain, spectral, or tipped; added 2026-10-04)
  is pulled from the death-drop list and silently returned
  to the player's inventory on respawn (overflow drops on the ground if full). Everything else drops normally.
- Bug fixed 2026-09-24: gear kept on death waited in memory only, so a player who died and quit without pressing respawn lost all of it if the server
  restarted while they were still on the death screen - it was never written to disk, and nothing cleaned it up for players who never came back
  either. It now waits on the player's own persistent data (their playerdata file) as well as in memory: memory serves the ordinary die-and-respawn
  case, the stored copy covers quitting on the death screen and any restart, and a stash that somehow outlives its respawn is handed back on the
  player's next join. Gear already lost to this can't be recovered.
- XP softened, not reset: vanilla resets level to 0 and spawns orbs worth up to min(level x 7, 100). This instead drops no orbs and subtracts a flat 3
  levels (floored at 0) - remaining progress toward the next level is kept, then reset to 0% within the new, reduced level.
- Slower despawn: items dropped from a player's death take twice as long to despawn - 12,000 ticks (10 min) instead of vanilla's 6,000 (5 min).
  Implemented by tagging each death drop and cancelling its first despawn; vanilla resets a cancelled item's age to 0, so it simply lives a second
  full cycle. Like any dropped item it only ages while its chunk is loaded, so 10 minutes is of loaded time, not wall clock.
  (Was silently broken until 2026-09-19: tagging hooked EntityDropItemEvent, which Paper never fires for death drops - nothing was ever tagged and
  every death drop vanished at vanilla's 5 minutes. Tagging now matches each spawning item against the death's own drop list.)

Mob XP bonus (MobXpListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Every mob drops 50% more XP on death (added 2026-10-02). Vanilla still decides whether a mob drops XP at all (e.g. it must have been killed by a
  player or tamed wolf), so mobs that drop nothing still drop nothing. Iron Golems stay at 0. Players' own death XP is unaffected.
- Most mobs drop small amounts, so the result is rounded up or down at random in proportion: a 5-XP mob drops 7 or 8, averaging exactly 7.5.
- The XP cap below still applies, so the bonus fills the 1000-point window faster rather than raising it.
- The Ender Dragon is boosted too (18,000 XP the first time, 750 after), since Paper spreads its XP over the death animation from the same amount.
- A kill with a Wisdom weapon (section 3) multiplies this further. Checked by "./gradlew mobXpCheck": 1-12,000 XP at every Wisdom level, 400,000
  rolls each, averaging exactly the intended amount and only ever landing on the two whole numbers either side of it.

XP gain cap (XpCapListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- New mechanic: each player can gain at most 1000 raw XP points (not levels) per 10-minute window (500 per 5 minutes until 2026-10-09 - the
  same average rate, in longer stretches). The windows are fixed and shared by everyone, set by the clock - they reset at :00, :10, :20 and so on
  past every hour - not a rolling 10 minutes from each player's own first gain. So up to 1000 can be earned just before a reset and another 1000
  just after it. The count is kept in memory only: relogging doesn't reset it, but a server
  restart does. Applies to any positive XP source routed through the vanilla point-grant event (mob kills, orb pickup, mining, breeding,
  fishing, furnace pickup, villager trading). "/xp add ... levels" and other direct level grants are a separate event this cap doesn't touch.
- Hitting the cap tells the player in chat, and logs a line to the console (added 2026-10-08), once per player per window, with the time left
  until the window resets - e.g. "Steve hit the 1000 XP cap; window resets in 7:13."
- Never silent after that (fixed 2026-10-11). The chat message is once per window, which left the cap silent for the rest of it - up to ten
  minutes of orbs vanishing with nothing to say why, and the same again after relogging. Now every orb or Mending repair the cap swallows shows
  "XP cap reached - resets in m:ss" above the hotbar (at most once a second), and a capped player who rejoins gets the chat message again. The
  chat message also now says outright that XP picked up before the reset is lost.
- Audit 2026-10-11, against the server code: the cap can only be reached by picking up an XP orb - that is the one place the server fires the
  event it listens to - so /xp, advancement rewards and level grants can't trip it. On a pickup, Mending is paid first and counted, and only
  the leftover reaches the cap, so no point of an orb is counted twice. No case was found of it triggering when it shouldn't; the fault was the
  silence above.
- Lifted for the first Ender Dragon (added 2026-10-11). The first dragon killed in an End drops 12,000 XP (18,000 with the mob XP bonus), nearly
  all of which the cap would throw away. The cap is lifted for players in that End from the killing blow until 60 seconds after the dragon's body
  disappears. Counted from the body, not the blow, because the dragon first flies to the portal and plays a 10-second death animation, and only
  releases its XP in the last 2.5 seconds of it - a minute from the blow would be part gone before any XP existed. XP gained while lifted isn't
  counted against the window. Players in the End are told when it lifts, when the 60 seconds start, and when the cap is back; the console logs
  the start and end. Later dragons (500 XP, 750 boosted) are capped as usual. Tested live 2026-10-11 on the test server: blow 17:02:18, body gone
  17:02:38, cap back 17:03:38, and exactly 18,000 XP in orbs on the ground.
- Mending counts toward the same 1000. XP an orb spends repairing Mending gear is added to the player's total; a repair that would go past the cap
  is cut down to what's left, and once the cap is reached Mending stops repairing until the window resets (the orb is still used up).
  Bug fixed 2026-10-02: vanilla spends an orb's XP on Mending before the player receives any of it, and the cap only saw what was left over - so
  XP put into repairs was never counted, and a capped player could keep mending without limit.

Sleep streaks (SleepStreakListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- New mechanic: sleeping through the night on consecutive nights builds a streak. Starting on the 2nd consecutive night, each additional night adds
  another 25% chance of a 20-minute thunderstorm rolling in right after everyone wakes (2nd = 25%, 3rd = 50%, 4th = 75%, 5th = guaranteed). A
  triggered storm - or a night that passes without a qualifying sleep - resets the streak to 0. (The step was 1/3 per night, guaranteed by night 4,
  until 2026-09-20.)
- The reset happens at sunrise on any unslept night, not lazily at the next sleep. (Until 2026-09-18 it was lazy: the stored streak was only ever
  touched by a sleep, so the storm odds were right but every announcement reported a stale, already-broken streak.)
- Straw beds (new in 26.3) are covered by all of this: they have no separate event, so they build the streak and are refused during a storm exactly
  like an ordinary bed.
- Entering a bed is also blocked outright during any thunderstorm (vanilla or plugin-triggered), so a player can't sleep through the punishment storm
  to keep the streak alive. Both the thunder and storm flags must be set, since the thunder flag alone can linger after a weather transition with a
  clear, bright sky - blocking sleep then looked like the plugin inventing a storm that wasn't there.
- Vanilla's daytime refusal message is reworded from "You can sleep only at night or during thunderstorms" to "You can sleep only at night", since
  this plugin makes the thunderstorm half untrue. Only that one message is replaced; other bed refusals are left alone.
- Chat/console announcements, to the affected world: the streak at each nightfall, an increase when a night is slept through, a reset when day breaks
  on an unslept night (only if there was a streak to lose), the storm rolling in, and that storm passing. A vanilla storm ending says nothing, because
  it didn't reset anything.
- Both streak announcements quote the storm odds that streak is buying, rather than leaving players to work them out: nightfall reports what sleeping
  tonight would risk ("Current sleep streak: 2. Sleeping tonight: 50% chance of a thunderstorm."), and a slept-through night reports what sleeping
  again tomorrow would. The tomorrow-night figure is omitted when that sleep just triggered a storm, since the streak is back to 0 and the storm
  message arrives a tick later anyway. Getting that right means the storm is rolled before the announcement is composed, not after it is sent.
- Day and night are read from the world clock (ticks 12523-23478, via the shared WorldClock helper), not sky brightness. Brightness is what Minecraft's own isDayTime() measures, and
  weather dims it: a daytime thunderstorm read as "night", and rain clearing at dusk read as a false sunrise that wiped a streak. Worlds with no
  day/night cycle (Nether, End) are skipped entirely.

  Diamond Shovel       5.5 / 1.0                      6.5 / 1.0
  Diamond Spear        4.0 / 0.95                     5.0 / 0.95
  Netherite Sword      8.0 / 1.6                      9.0 / 1.6

Applied at crafting, on pickup, and re-verified against every item in a player's inventory on join - checked against the actual stored modifier

Unbreaking enchantment (UnbreakingListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Vanilla's damage-negation chance is 100 / (level + 1) for tools and weapons; this replaces it with a shallower curve, 100 / (1 + level / 3):

  Unbreaking level   Vanilla chance to still take damage   New chance
  I                  50%                                    75%
  II                 33.3%                                  60%
  III                25%                                    50%

Vanilla uses a second, shallower curve for armour - 1 - 2L/(5L+5), i.e. 80% / 73.3% / 70% - which the same replacement curve above now also applies
to, so armour is slightly better off at level I and worse off at level III than vanilla.

- Applies to every item Unbreaking can go on and every action that costs durability - bows, crossbows, tridents, shields, fishing rods, shears, flint
  & steel and brushes included, alongside the tools, weapons, armour and Elytra that were always covered. Mob-held gear too, through its own event.
- How, since 2026-09-20: the plugin ships a datapack, bundled inside its own jar, that replaces minecraft:unbreaking with a copy that has no
  item_damage effect. Vanilla then reduces nothing, its durability event fires on every single use, and the listener applies the curve literally -
  each point of damage is kept with the probability in the table above. Unbreaking II really is "60% chance to take the point".
- This is why the plugin is now a Paper plugin rather than a Bukkit one: registering a datapack needs a bootstrapper, which paper-plugin.yml supports
  and plugin.yml does not. The pack is auto-enabled on start and appears as "VanillaKFC/vanillakfc"; the startup log states which mode is running.
- Fallback if the datapack is ever disabled: the listener scales instead. Vanilla only raises its event for damage that survived its own roll - a roll
  of zero skips the event entirely - so a plugin cannot make damage land more often than vanilla decided, only change the number it is handed.
  Multiplying by (new curve / vanilla curve) puts the expected loss per use on the same curve: x1.5 / x1.8 / x2.0 for tools, x0.9375 / x0.8182 /
  x0.7143 for armour. The enabled flag is checked per event, not cached, so toggling the pack takes effect immediately - applying the curve on top of
  a reduction vanilla already made would roughly double an item's intended life.
- Both paths hit the same average; they differ in spread, which is the entire point of the datapack. Scaling inherits vanilla's coin flip and
  amplifies it by the multiplier, giving about three times the variance on tools. A golden pickaxe with Unbreaking II averages ~54 blocks either way,
  but the middle 95% of them runs 43-66 direct against 35-78 scaled. Low-durability tools feel it most, having the fewest rolls to average out.
  (Armour is the exception and is marginally tighter scaled, since its multiplier is below 1 and so damps rather than amplifies.)
- Vanilla's own rules still apply and simply arrive as a number to adjust: swords costing 2 durability per block broken, armour taking a fraction of
  the hit, an Elytra costing 1 per second airborne, and an item breaking at 0.
- Checked by "./gradlew unbreakingCheck", which runs the listener's own arithmetic for both paths against an independent transcription of both curves
  over a million simulated uses per case. It does not cover the event wiring or the armour tag lookup, which need a running server.
- Bug fixed 2026-09-20: before any of this, nothing outside a fixed list of four actions took durability damage at all. The original design cancelled
  vanilla's durability event for every Unbreaking item and re-applied the loss by hand from block-break, melee, armour-damage and Elytra handlers, so
  anything no handler reached had its damage cancelled with nothing putting it back. An Unbreaking bow was simply indestructible, as were crossbows,
  tridents, shields, fishing rods, shears, flint & steel and brushes.

Wisdom (bundled datapack: data/vanillakfc/enchantment/wisdom.json; MobXpListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Added 2026-10-08. A new melee weapon enchantment, levels I-III.
- Kills made with it drop more XP: +15% / +30% / +50% at I / II / III, multiplied on top of the plugin's 1.5x mob XP bonus rather than added to
  it - 1.725x / 1.95x / 2.25x vanilla in all. A 5-XP zombie averages 8.6 / 9.75 / 11.25 instead of 7.5. Vanilla still decides whether a mob drops
  XP at all, and the 1000-XP-per-10-minutes cap still applies.
- Read from the main hand of the player credited with the kill, at the moment of death - the same slot Looting works from - so a bow kill only
  counts if the Wisdom weapon is in the main hand. Kills credited to a tamed wolf get no Wisdom bonus.
- Mutually exclusive with Looting.
- Same rarity and costs as Looting (weight 2, level I from power 15, each level 9 apart), on the same items (#minecraft:enchantable/melee_weapon),
  so in an enchanting table its levels come up at Looting's own odds. Simulated on a diamond sword with vanilla's enchanting code against this
  server's data, levels I / II / III per enchant: table level 20 - Looting 6.2% / 4.4% / 0%, Wisdom 6.1% / 4.3% / 0%; table level 30 - Looting 0%
  / 5.6% / 8.2%, Wisdom 0% / 5.4% / 8.0%. Averaged over table levels 1-30, both 2.8% / 3.4% / 0.7%.
- Obtained like any normal enchantment: enchanting table, anvil books, librarian trades (generic book pool), and loot.

Deep Quiver (bundled datapack: data/vanillakfc/enchantment/deep_quiver.json)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Added 2026-10-04. A new enchantment for bows and crossbows, levels I-III.
- Each shot has a chance not to use up its arrow: 25% / 40% / 50% at I / II / III - the mirror of this plugin's Unbreaking, which keeps
  75% / 60% / 50% of durability damage. Works on whichever arrow the game would fire anyway (offhand first, then the inventory), plain or tipped
  or spectral. For a crossbow the roll is made when it's loaded, which is when a crossbow uses its ammo; Multishot still only ever costs one.
- No arrow fired from a Deep Quiver bow or crossbow can ever be picked back up, whether it hits something or not and whether or not the shot
  used one up - every shot behaves like an Infinity arrow (DeepQuiverListener). Changed 2026-10-09: it used to be only the arrows that weren't
  used up, which is vanilla's own rule, so a shot that did use one left an arrow that could be collected and fired again.
- Mutually exclusive with Infinity and Mending (vanilla's own bow exclusive set, which already keeps those two apart), so a bow or crossbow can
  carry only one of the three.
- Obtained like any normal enchantment: enchanting table, anvil books, librarian trades (it's in the generic book pool), and loot. Weight 1, as
  rare as Infinity (lowered from 2, Punch and Flame's, on 2026-10-08); enchanting costs as Unbreaking's.
- The ammo chance is in the datapack, on vanilla's own ammo-use effect (what Infinity is built on), set to 0 by a level-based random chance; the
  no-pickup rule above is the plugin's.

Sharper Fangs (bundled datapack: fire_protection.json, thorns.json, unbreaking.json; SharperFangsListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Added 2026-10-04. Fire Protection is gone as an armor enchantment; in its place is "Sharper Fangs", for wolf armor. (It started out the same day
as "Wolf Protection", a I-IV all-round damage reduction - see the last two points for what became of it.)
- Same enchantment underneath (still minecraft:fire_protection), redefined: goes only on wolf armor, has a single level (like Mending or
  Infinity), and gives the wolf wearing it +4 attack damage - an attribute modifier from the body slot, so it lasts exactly as long as the armor
  is on. It stacks on top of the tamed-wolf x1.5 (which scales the wolf's base damage), so an armored tamed wolf hits for 4 x 1.5 + 4 = 10
  instead of 6. Fire Protection's fire reduction and burning-time reduction are both gone.
- Rarity is Fire Protection's (weight 5); it can come up in the enchanting table at any power (1-51), so every wolf armor slot always has
  something to offer alongside Thorns and Unbreaking. (Fixed 2026-10-04, as Wolf Protection: it first kept Fire Protection's costs, which start
  at power 10, and a wolf armor's cheap table slot usually rolled too low for anything and showed up empty, doing nothing when clicked.)
- No longer on regular armor by any route: enchanting table, anvil books, villager armor trades, loot or mob equipment.
- Existing enchanted books need no re-id - being the same enchantment, every Fire Protection or Wolf Protection book now reads Sharper Fangs.
  Desert librarians, whose pool included Fire Protection, now offer Sharper Fangs books in its place, as does the generic pool; new offers are
  always level I.
- Thorns and Unbreaking can now go on wolf armor too (book or table). Thorns treats wolf armor like a chestplate, so it can come up in the table.
  Mending is deliberately still excluded - nothing would ever mend wolf armor, since it is never in a player's equipment.
- Enchanting table: wolf armor now works in one, with iron armor's enchantability (9), so its rolls - Sharper Fangs, Thorns, Unbreaking - come
  out at the same odds iron armor gets. Vanilla wolf armor has no enchantability and a datapack can't add one, so the plugin gives it to each piece:
  when crafted (crafting table or crafter), during the conversion pass below, and when put into an enchanting table, as a catch-all.
- One-time conversion of existing items: armor (or anything else other than a book or wolf armor) carrying Fire Protection has it replaced with
  Protection at the same level, and books and wolf armor carrying it above level I (left over from Wolf Protection's I-IV, which the game
  doesn't lower by itself) are set to I - the bonus is the same at any level, so this is only so they read right. It happens as things load:
  containers and entities (dropped items, item frames, mob and armor stand equipment - armored wolves included - minecart/boat/animal inventories,
  villager trade offers) when their chunk loads, and inventories and ender chests when a player joins - each chunk and player only once. Shulker
  boxes and bundles are searched inside. Each batch converted is logged. The pass was restarted for Sharper Fangs, so chunks and players already
  covered for Wolf Protection are gone over once more.

Bane of the Living (bundled datapack: data/minecraft/enchantment/bane_of_arthropods.json)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Bane of Arthropods is renamed "Bane of the Living" and reworked. It now adds +1.5 damage per level (1.5 / 3 / 4.5 / 6 / 7.5 for I-V) against every
  mob that Smite doesn't boost - i.e. anything not in #minecraft:sensitive_to_smite (all skeletons and zombies, the Wither, phantoms). Vanilla's
  +2.5/level only hit arthropods.
- Players are excluded: the bonus is for mobs only, so it does nothing in PvP.
- Vanilla's Slowness IV on hitting an arthropod is removed - arthropods simply take the same +1.5/level as every other living mob.
- Unchanged: max level V, same enchanting table/anvil costs and weight, same weapons, and still mutually exclusive with Sharpness and Smite.
- The name is sent to clients with the rest of the enchantment registry, so every player sees it (no resource pack needed) in tooltips, the
  enchanting table, anvils and trades. It's plain text rather than a translation key, so it reads "Bane of the Living" in every client language.
  The internal id is still minecraft:bane_of_arthropods, which is what commands like /enchant take, so existing enchanted items carry over as-is.

Mending repair rate (bundled datapack: data/minecraft/enchantment/mending.json)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Added 2026-10-09.
- Mending restores 1 durability per XP point instead of vanilla's 2, so repairing the same damage takes twice the XP. Everything else about it
  is vanilla - which item gets repaired, the leftover XP going to the player, where it can be found.
- With the plugin's doubled-and-more durabilities and the XP cap (1000 per 10 minutes, which Mending draws from), one window's XP now mends at most
  1000 durability rather than 2000.
- Done by overriding Mending's own definition with vanilla's, changing only the repair multiplier from 2.0 to 1.0. Disabling the "VanillaKFC"
  datapack reverts it.

Elytra & Mending (ElytraEnchantListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Mending can no longer end up on an Elytra by any means - stripped if produced by combining at an anvil, and defensively stripped if it's ever
  somehow rolled at an enchanting table.
- Fixed 2026-10-13 - a Mending book was eaten for nothing: an Elytra plus a Mending book still showed a result, the same Elytra without Mending,
  and taking it cost the book and the levels and raised the Elytra's anvil penalty. The anvil now gives no result whenever Mending is all the second
  item would add - a Mending book, or an undamaged Elytra combined with a Mending Elytra - renamed in the same go or not. If the second item does

Anvil repair-with-material (AnvilRepairListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- For iron/gold/diamond tools, weapons, and armor: vanilla's "1 ingot restores 25% durability" is fully replaced. Ingots no longer repair
  these items - instead, 1 block of the matching tier material (Iron/Gold/Diamond Block) restores 75% durability, consuming as many blocks
  as needed (up to what's available, so at most 2).
- Netherite tools, weapons, and armor take a Netherite Ingot instead of a block: 1 ingot restores 75%, on the same terms otherwise (at most 2 per
  repair, same penalty and level cost). Netherite Blocks no longer repair them. Changed 2026-10-04
  (previously a Netherite Block, like the other tiers).
- Repairing and combining have separate penalties (changed 2026-10-22). Vanilla keeps one hidden prior-work penalty per item that every anvil use
  charges and raises, so a much-enchanted item was expensive to repair and a much-repaired one expensive to enchant. Now every material repair
  (blocks, Netherite Ingots, Hearts of the Sea, Phantom Membranes) charges and raises a repair penalty of its own, stored on the item, and leaves
  vanilla's penalty alone. Combining (enchanted books, or two items - including two damaged items) uses only vanilla's penalty, priced and raised
  exactly as vanilla does (n to 2n+1). Neither penalty is ever charged for the other kind of work.
- That covers vanilla's own material repairs too - copper, leather, wood and stone gear, chainmail, shields, the mace, turtle shells, wolf armor
  and anything else repaired with a material - which keep vanilla's 25% per unit but are priced on the repair penalty like everything above.
- Combining two items keeps the higher of each penalty: the result's repair penalty is the higher of the two items' (combining doesn't raise it),
  and its combining penalty is the higher of the two, then raised once for this combine, as vanilla does. E.g. a pickaxe with 3 combines and 1
  repair merged with one with 1 combine and 4 repairs comes out with 4 repairs and 4 combines (the merge itself is the 4th).
- A material repair costs the repair penalty plus 1 level per unit of material consumed, and takes the repair penalty from n to ceil(1.5n + 1):
  from new, 0, 1, 3, 6, 10, 16, 25, 39, so an item gets 7 repairs before "Too Expensive!" (vanilla's 0, 1, 3, 7, 15, 31 allowed 6). Before
  2026-10-22 block repairs raised the shared penalty from n to n + ceil(1.5 x (n+1)) (0, 2, 7, 19, 49 - only 4 repairs); deliberately cut back.
- One-time reset of existing items: the first time an item from before the split goes in an anvil (the result shows a tick later than usual that
  once), both its penalties start over at 0, whatever it had built up - so anything that was "Too Expensive!" can be worked again. Its old single
  number mixed repairing and combining with no record of which was which, so there was nothing reliable to split it by.
- A grindstone still resets vanilla's penalty as usual (it strips the enchantments that built it) but leaves the repair penalty alone.
- Tridents can be repaired with a Heart of the Sea, 75% of durability each - 300 of the trident's 400 (added 2026-09-25). Purely an
  addition: vanilla has no repair material for a trident at all, so combining two tridents and Mending were the only options, and both still work.
- An Elytra is fully repaired by a single Phantom Membrane, however damaged it is (added 2026-10-13). Vanilla's restores 25% each, so a worn-out
  Elytra took 4. Only one membrane is ever used, whatever the size of the stack in the anvil. Since 2026-10-22 it's priced like the other material
  repairs - repair penalty plus 1 level, same growth, 7 repairs - where it used vanilla's shared penalty and growth before. Renaming in the same
  operation works, unless the Elytra's combining penalty alone would make vanilla call it "Too Expensive!", in which case it's repaired without
  the new name. Mending is still stripped from an Elytra repaired this way.
- Two long-standing bugs fixed alongside that (2026-09-19): block repairs never raised the penalty at all (the result was a straight copy of the
  input), and the level cost ignored the item's anvil history entirely, charging 1 level per block however worn the item was. Vanilla's "Too
  Expensive!" refusal at 40 levels was also being skipped, because it is checked before the plugin substitutes its own result; it is now re-applied
  (creative exempt).
- Known gap, unchanged: renaming an item in the same operation as a block repair still discards the new name.

Free anvil renaming (AnvilRenameListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Added 2026-10-22. Renaming at an anvil no longer costs XP.
- A rename on its own costs 0 levels, however high the item's prior-work penalty (vanilla charges the penalty plus 1 level, capped at 39). Taking
  off a custom name counts as a rename and is free too.
- A rename done alongside combining no longer adds its 1 level; the rest of the operation costs what it did. Material repairs never charge for a
  name (a block repair drops the new name anyway - see the known gap above).
- Neither penalty is changed by renaming: a rename on its own leaves both where they were (as vanilla already does), and alongside other work the
  penalty for that work grows once, the same as without a new name.
- Edge case: vanilla decides "Too Expensive!" before the plugin takes the rename's level back off, so an operation that only goes over the limit
  because of the rename is still refused. Doing the rename separately gets around it.


4. CRAFTING & RECIPES (CustomRecipes, RecipeDiscoveryListener)
==================================================================
- Tipped arrows (added 2026-10-04): 8 Arrows around a Splash Potion, instead of vanilla's Lingering Potion, make 8 Tipped Arrows of that potion.
  Bundled datapack override of minecraft:tipped_arrow; its recipe-book unlock now triggers on holding a Splash Potion. Lingering potions no longer
  make tipped arrows. Fletchers still sell them.
- Ingot -> block gear recipes: every iron, gold, and diamond tool, weapon, armor piece, and the Shield now use a block of the tier material instead of
  loose ingots/gems - 1 block per ingot slot for tools/weapons/shield, 2 blocks for armor pieces. Every valid slot arrangement is registered as its
  own recipe variant, so the block can go in any of the original ingot slots.
- Enchanting Table reworked the same way: both of its Diamonds become Diamond Blocks (the Book and 4 Obsidian are unchanged). It keeps vanilla's Misc
  recipe-book tab rather than the Equipment tab the gear recipes use.
- One recipe-book entry per item: only the first slot arrangement is ever shown. The others stay fully craftable but are never revealed, so a pickaxe
  occupies one book entry instead of one per block position. Of 255 registered recipes, 201 are hidden alternates, leaving 54 book entries. Minecraft
  draws one fixed shape per recipe and offers no way to cycle a single entry through several, so this consolidates the display rather than rotating
  it. Crafting via a hidden arrangement does not add it to the book.
- Recipes now appear in the book once a player is holding the ingredients, like vanilla's own. Vanilla drives that off per-recipe advancements that
  only exist for minecraft:-namespaced recipes, so the 237 recipes registered under this plugin's namespace were previously invisible until a player
  happened to craft one by hand - which is what made them show up "only after making one". Players carrying alternates unlocked by the older build get
  them cleared on join.
- Netherite Block recipe rebuilt as a fixed 2x2 shape (top-left of the grid) of Netherite Ingots.
- Netherite smithing upgrades switched to Block: every smithing-table upgrade recipe now takes a Netherite Block as its "addition" instead of an
  ingot.
- Chain output doubled (vanilla's ingredients/shape otherwise untouched).
- Shulker Box recipe changed (added 2026-10-09; bundled datapack, data/minecraft/recipe/shulker_box.json): a Chest surrounded by 8 Shulker Shells,
  instead of vanilla's 2 (one above the chest, one below). Same recipe id, so it unlocks in the recipe book the same way as before. Dyeing a
  shulker box is unchanged.
- Chainmail armor recipes added: no vanilla recipe exists for any of the 4 pieces; this adds one for each, mirroring other armor tiers' shapes with
  Chains filling every ingredient slot.
- Leather armor reworked: all 4 recipes replaced - instead of leather-only, each needs Leather (or Rotten Flesh, 1-for-1) plus String and Wood Planks
  (any type), using the same L/S/P shapes as other armor tiers.
- Green dye from yellow + blue (bundled datapack): a shapeless yellow dye + blue dye now makes 2 green dye, matching how orange (red + yellow) and
  purple (red + blue) already work in vanilla. Vanilla has no crafting recipe for green at all - smelting cactus is the only source - so this adds
  one rather than replacing anything, and cactus smelting still works. Registered under minecraft:green_dye_from_yellow_blue with a matching recipe
  advancement, the same pair of files vanilla ships for its own dye mixes, so it unlocks in the recipe book as soon as a player holds either dye.
  Datapack rather than plugin code, since it needs none of the slot-arrangement machinery the gear recipes above use.
- Recipe book category fixed: every rebuilt recipe above is explicitly placed in the Equipment tab. A freshly constructed recipe defaults to Misc, and
  since each fully replaces the vanilla recipe under its original key, leaving the default would have silently moved them all to Misc.


5. VILLAGER TRADING - LIBRARIANS (VillagerTradeListener)
==================================================================
Every librarian trade for levels 1-4 (Novice-Expert), plus a second enchanted book at level 5 (Master), is generated directly by this listener - no
bundled datapack. Vanilla's own trade-unlock pipeline still fires as a librarian levels up, but any
enchanted-book trade it tries to add is cancelled outright at every level (see safety net below); the listener tops up whatever's missing for the
villager's current level each time a player opens its trade menu.

- Novice (1): always has both "24 Paper -> 1 Emerald" (16 uses) and "9 Emerald -> 1 Bookshelf" (12 uses) - matching vanilla exactly, with vanilla's
  own generic enchanted-book candidate (normally also in this pool) kept out.
- Apprentice (2): always has both "4 Book -> 1 Emerald" (12 uses) and "1 Emerald -> 1 Lantern" (12 uses).
- Journeyman (3):
    - Always exactly one of "5 Ink Sac -> 1 Emerald" or "1 Emerald -> 4 Glass" (12 uses), chosen once per villager and fixed for life.
    - Always guaranteed an enchanted-book trade, chosen once and fixed for life (reaching level 5 doesn't re-roll it). With trade_rebalance enabled,
      the enchant is drawn with even odds from a biome-specific pool: that biome's 2-3 regular enchants (level 1-2, 5-32 emeralds) plus its
      strongest/"Master"-tier enchant (table below) as one more equally-likely option, at its fixed level and vanilla's higher master-tier price
      (11-36 emeralds at level 3, 8-26 lower). With trade_rebalance disabled, the enchant is instead drawn uniformly from the generic pool below,
      with no biome flavor and no fixed-level entries - same as vanilla's non-biome-specific trades.
    - Either way, every regular-tier book (i.e. everything except a biome's one folded-in strongest enchant) is level 1 or 2 and costs 5-32 emeralds,
      capped at the enchantment's own max level - so Silk Touch or Channeling can only ever be level 1.
    - The generic pool is read live from the server's own #minecraft:in_enchanting_table tag rather than written out in the plugin, so it is exactly
      what an enchanting table could offer, and a new enchantment is picked up automatically. Mending and both curses are treasure enchantments and so
      are absent by construction. Changed 2026-09-27: it used to be a hand-kept list, which had fallen behind - it was missing Breach, Density and
      Lunge, and included Frost Walker, which an enchanting table cannot give.
- Expert (4):
    - Always guaranteed a "2 Writable Books -> 1 Emerald" trade.
    - Never offers a plain enchanted-book trade.
    - Always exactly one of "5 Emerald -> 1 Clock" or "4 Emerald -> 1 Compass" (12 uses), chosen once and fixed thereafter.
- Master (5) (added 2026-10-05):
    - Always guaranteed a second enchanted-book trade, on top of the level 3 one (which is kept). Rolled by exactly the same rules as the level 3 book
      - same pool (biome-specific or generic, per trade_rebalance), same odds, same levels and prices, same villager xp - except it never repeats an
      enchantment the librarian already sells. Chosen once and fixed for life.
    - Librarians already at Master get theirs the next time a player opens their trade menu.
    - Vanilla's own Master trades (e.g. the Name Tag) are unaffected.
- Each biome's folded-in strongest enchant is checked directly against this Minecraft version's actual trade_rebalance data (extracted from the server
  jar). Swamp is changed from vanilla's Mending to Thorns II; the other six match vanilla exactly:

    Biome        Strongest enchantment
    Desert       Efficiency III
    Jungle       Unbreaking II
    Plains       Protection III
    Savanna      Sharpness III
    Snow         Silk Touch I
    Swamp        Thorns II  (vanilla: Mending)
    Taiga        Fortune II

- Safety net, all levels: any enchanted-book trade vanilla's own trade-unlock pipeline tries to add (base pool, or the trade_rebalance overlay if
  enabled) is cancelled outright - every enchanted-book trade a librarian ends up with comes from this listener alone (this is what fixed the original
  "enchanted book at Novice level" bug).
- Curses excluded: Curse of Binding and Curse of Vanishing are in no pool, and any curse-enchanted book a librarian already has (from before this
  rule, or another source) is stripped and replaced the next time its trade menu opens. Frost Walker stays tradeable, but only in Snow's biome pool -
  it's a treasure enchantment, not a curse, so it left the generic pool when that became the enchanting-table tag. Mending is never in either pool.

Diamond gear trade prices (DiamondTradePriceListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Any villager trade selling a diamond sword, spear, pickaxe, axe, shovel, hoe, helmet, chestplate, leggings or boots costs double: every ingredient's
  count is doubled (emeralds, and diamonds too where a trade asks for them). Enchanted versions included. Added 2026-09-29.
- Doubled on the base price, so vanilla's discounts (curing, Hero of the Village, reputation) and demand surcharges still apply on top and scale with it.
- Capped at 64: an ingredient whose doubled count would pass 64 costs 64 instead (e.g. a 40-emerald trade becomes 64, not 80).
- New trades are doubled as a villager unlocks them. Villagers that already had diamond trades get a one-time pass - at startup, when their chunk
  loads, or when their trade menu is opened, whichever comes first - and are then marked (diamond_trades_doubled) so nothing is ever doubled twice.
- Bug fixed 2026-09-29: every doubled trade showed as sold out ("needs to restock") from the moment it appeared. The doubled copy was made with
  Paper's MerchantRecipe copy constructor, which reads raw fields the server's own trades leave at 0 - so each copy had 0 max uses, and also lost its
  villager XP and demand pricing. Copies are now built from the trade's real values. Trades already broken this way are rebuilt when the villager is
  next loaded or its menu opened: 3 uses (vanilla's for every diamond gear trade), villager XP 30 for sword/pickaxe/helmet/chestplate, 10 for the
  hoe, 15 for the rest, and vanilla's price multiplier. The doubled price is kept.

Cartographers (bundled datapack: data/minecraft/tags/villager_trade/cartographer/level_3.json)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Added 2026-10-09.
- Journeyman (level 3) cartographers always offer both the Ocean Explorer Map (monument) and the Trial Explorer Map (trial chambers), at vanilla's
  prices. Vanilla picks 2 of 3 trades at this level, the third being "1 Compass -> 1 Emerald"; that trade is removed from the pool, leaving
  exactly the two maps for the two picks.
- A map trade still needs its structure to be findable: vanilla searches around the villager when the trade is made and drops the trade if
  nothing turns up (e.g. no ocean monument anywhere near an inland village). That's unchanged, so in that rare case only the other map appears.
- Applies to cartographers reaching level 3 from now on. Ones already at level 3 or above keep the trades they rolled.


6. FARMING & FOOD
==================================================================

Crop growth (CropGrowthListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Applies to everything in vanilla's own #minecraft:crops tag - Wheat, Carrots, Potatoes, Beetroot, Pumpkin/Melon stems, and (added 2026-09-19)
Torchflower and Pitcher crops, which an older hand-kept list had missed. Nether Wart is not in that tag and stays excluded, as do plants that don't
grow on farmland (sugar cane, cactus, bamboo, berries, cocoa, kelp):
- Half growth rate at night and during thunderstorms - vanilla grows on random ticks regardless of time or weather. This was a total block until
  2026-09-18; halving it instead cut the time for a full field by ~29%.
- Night is taken from the world clock (ticks 12523-23478, via the shared WorldClock helper), not sky brightness, so ordinary rain around dusk no
  longer counts as night. Thunderstorms
  are penalised explicitly instead.
- Requires a clear sky (sky light 15 in the column above) - glass, trapdoors, and bars still allow growth, but any solid roof blocks it entirely.
  Vanilla farmland crops don't need sky access at all.
- No crop may run more than 2 growth stages ahead of the least-grown crop of the same type within 4 blocks; one sitting at that trailing edge grows at
  full rate. This is what keeps a field maturing together: vanilla growth is a per-block lottery, so a field planted in one go finishes raggedly.
  Simulated over a solid 9x9 hydrated wheat plot with the chunk held loaded, the gap between the first and last crop maturing falls from ~88 min to
  ~46 min, with the full field taking ~186 min against vanilla's ~103. The cost is that nothing ripens early any more: the first crop now lands at
  ~137 min instead of ~15.
- Consequence worth knowing: one crop replanted into an otherwise mature field counts as the trailing edge and briefly holds the rest back. It
  resolves on its own, since trailing crops grow at full rate.
- The old "2/3 of growth rolls kept" thinning is retired (set to 1.0). Once the neighbour cap existed it was worth only ~10% of the field's time,
  because trailing crops skip the thinning roll and most crops sit at the trailing edge most of the time.
- Bone meal is entirely exempt and behaves exactly as in vanilla - no sky requirement, no night penalty, and it can push a crop past its neighbours.
  (Until 2026-09-19 every rule above applied to bone meal too, since Paper reports bone-meal growth through the same event as natural growth; the
  neighbour cap refused it outright on any crop already 2 stages ahead.)
- Unaffected: a stem's Pumpkin or Melon still appears at vanilla's rate once the stem is mature, because that growth is reported against the empty
  block the fruit fills, not the stem.

Animal breeding growth (BreedingGrowthListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Baby animals produced by breeding (not wild-spawned) take 3x as long to mature - their starting negative age counter is multiplied by 3 at birth.
  Vanilla's ~20-minute maturation becomes ~60 minutes under this plugin.

Food saturation (FoodSaturationListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Doubles the saturation value (satiety duration, not nutrition/hunger restored) of the 5 breedable-animal meats (raw and cooked) plus plain
farmland-crop foods and bread:

  Food              Vanilla saturation   New saturation
  Beef (raw)        1.8                   3.6
  Cooked Beef       12.8                  25.6
  Porkchop (raw)    1.8                   3.6
  Cooked Porkchop   12.8                  25.6
  Chicken (raw)     1.2                   2.4
  Cooked Chicken    7.2                   14.4
  Mutton (raw)      1.2                   2.4
  Cooked Mutton     9.6                   19.2
  Rabbit (raw)      1.8                   3.6
  Cooked Rabbit     6.0                   12.0
  Carrot            3.6                   7.2
  Potato            0.6                   1.2
  Baked Potato      6.0                   12.0
  Beetroot          1.2                   2.4
  Melon Slice       1.2                   2.4
  Bread             6.0                   12.0

Fish, rotten flesh, composite dishes (pumpkin pie, beetroot soup, stews), and non-farmland foods (apples, berries, golden carrot) are unaffected.


7. COSMETIC / NON-MECHANICAL ADDITIONS

Custom Enderman dodge sound (EndermanDodgeSoundPackListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Hosts a tiny resource pack (a self-run local HTTP server) that adds three new, purely additive sound events: one played when an Enderman's
dodge-teleport lands with the "behind the player" bias, one (added 2026-10-11) played when an Enderman turns hostile and teleports in next to
the player, and one (added 2026-10-11) played to a player arriving in the End while a dragon is alive. It never overrides any vanilla sound and layers safely alongside a player's own resource packs.
- Host and port come from config.yml (resource-pack-host, resource-pack-port; defaults 127.0.0.1 and 25566). The default only reaches players
  connecting from the same machine the server runs on - a public deployment needs a reachable host set here and that port opened.
- Pack format declares 97 (26.3) and accepts formats 88 upwards. It previously pinned max_format to 88 (26.2 only), which made 26.3 clients reject the
  whole pack: every vanilla Enderman sound kept working while this one silently never played.
- Serves up to 8 downloads at once and drops a connection that takes over 15s to send its request or 120s to finish its download (fixed
  2026-10-18). Before, downloads ran one at a time with no time limit, so one stalled connection - a player who dropped mid-download, or a port
  scanner - quietly left every later player without the pack (no sounds, vanilla shield) until the server restarted.

Charged creeper death messages (ChargedCreeperDeathMessageListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Added 2026-10-04. A player killed by a charged creeper's death message names it "Charged Creeper" instead of vanilla's plain "Creeper" (e.g.
"Steve was blown up by Charged Creeper"). Hovering the name still shows the creeper, as in vanilla, and a creeper with a custom name keeps it.
- Covers deaths that name the creeper after the fact too, such as "was doomed to fall by Charged Creeper" after a blast knocks a player off a
  ledge: charged creepers are noted the moment they go off and remembered for a minute, longer than vanilla keeps an attacker in a player's
  combat history.
- "Charged Creeper" is plain English text, not translated - unlike vanilla's "Creeper", which each client shows in its own language.

Custom advancements (bundled datapack, DeathMilestoneListener, ChargedCreeperKillListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
A "VanillaKFC" advancement tab, in the plugin's own vanillakfc: namespace, so nothing here touches or overrides a vanilla advancement.
- vanillakfc:root - the tab itself. Completes on its own the first tick a player is in the world (minecraft:tick trigger), so the tab is always
  visible and everything under it is reachable from the start. Silent: no toast, no chat announcement.
- vanillakfc:dying_grace - "Dying Grace", awarded at 40 deaths. Challenge frame, toast and chat announcement on, Totem of Undying icon.
- vanillakfc:great_scott - "Great Scott!", awarded at 200 deaths. Challenge frame, toast and chat announcement on, Clock icon. Counted and awarded
  exactly as Dying Grace is (see below), from the same death count; a player already past 200 gets both on joining. Added 2026-10-13.
- vanillakfc:oh_neptune - "Oh Neptune", awarded for repairing a trident with a Heart of the Sea (see section 3). Challenge frame, toast and chat
  announcement on, Heart of the Sea icon. Awarded when the repaired trident is actually taken out of the anvil and the Heart is gone from it, not when
  the anvil merely previews the result - there is no "anvil used" event, so the check is whether the material was consumed, read a tick after the
  click (at click time nothing has been applied yet, and the take can still fail on levels or on "Too Expensive!").
- vanillakfc:fallen_angel - "Fallen Angel", awarded for killing a charged creeper. Challenge frame, toast and chat announcement on, glinting
  Creeper Head icon. Credit goes to whoever vanilla credits with the kill, so ranged kills count; a charged creeper that blows itself up counts
  for nobody. Re-testable with "/advancement revoke <player> only vanillakfc:fallen_angel".
- vanillakfc:copper_is_the_new_iron - "Copper is the new Iron", awarded for crafting any copper tool or weapon (pickaxe, axe, shovel, hoe, sword
  or spear). Regular task frame, toast and chat announcement on, Copper Pickaxe icon. Datapack-only (vanilla's recipe_crafted trigger), so it fires
  from a crafting table or the 2x2 grid but not from a Crafter, which has no player to credit. Added 2026-10-04.
- vanillakfc:prison_break - "Prison Break", awarded for killing a Warden. Challenge frame, toast and chat announcement on, Sculk Shrieker icon.
  Datapack-only (vanilla's player_killed_entity trigger), so credit goes to whoever vanilla credits with the kill - ranged kills count, as do kills
  by a tamed wolf. Re-testable with "/advancement revoke <player> only vanillakfc:prison_break". Added 2026-10-04.
- The death count is read from the server's own scoreboard: any objective whose criterion is deathCount, whatever the objective is called, so it works
  with an existing counter as-is. The advancement
  then agrees with the number players already see on a sidebar. The highest such objective wins if there are several. (The first count read each
  session used to be logged, naming the objective it came from; removed 2026-10-09.) With no deathCount objective on the
  scoreboard at all, it falls back to the player's own DEATHS statistic, which vanilla always tracks. Note a sidebar fed by a plain "dummy" objective
  is not a death counter as far as this is concerned and will land on the statistic instead.
- Checked one tick after a death (vanilla increments the scoreboard after the death event, so reading it during the event is one behind) and again on
  join, so anyone already past 40 (or 200) earns it on their way in rather than needing one more death.
- Each death advancement holds a single minecraft:impossible criterion - vanilla can count deaths in a session but not a running total, so the plugin
  decides when it is earned. Re-testable with "/advancement revoke <player> only vanillakfc:dying_grace" (or vanillakfc:great_scott).

Lowered first-person shield (resource pack, same pack as above)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Held normally in the main hand or offhand, the shield sits slightly lower in first person than vanilla, so it covers a little less of the
screen. Width, tilt and scale are unchanged; it's only moved down.
- Blocking is untouched: raising the shield switches to vanilla's separate shield_blocking model, which the pack doesn't override, so the blocking
  view is exactly vanilla.
- Third person, inventory/GUI, item frames, shelves and dropped shields are all unchanged - the pack's shield.json copies vanilla's values for every
  display slot except firstperson_righthand/lefthand, whose Y translation drops by 4 (1.75 -> -2.25 right, 0 -> -4 left).
- Visual only, and only for players who accept the (optional) pack. Unlike the sound, this one does override a vanilla asset, so a player's own pack
  that also replaces models/item/shield.json will win or lose depending on pack order.


8. WORLD / DIMENSIONS
==================================================================

Nether portal scaling (NetherPortalScaleListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Vanilla links the Nether and Overworld at an 8:1 ratio (1 Nether block = 8 Overworld blocks); this is rewritten to a flat 1:1 ratio. The X/Z an
  entity stands on when stepping through a portal is the X/Z it arrives at on the other side, unscaled, still clamped to the destination world's
  border. Target world, Y, and facing are left untouched - only X/Z scaling changes.
- End portals are unaffected - the End's coordinate_scale already matches the Overworld's in vanilla.

Nether roof void damage (NetherRoofVoidDamageListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- New mechanic: the Nether's bedrock roof can never generate above Y=127 (derived from the dimension's noise settings - min_y 0, height 128, roof
  carved 6 blocks from the top, matching logical_height of 128). Any player at Y >= 128 while in the Nether takes vanilla's out-of-world damage (4
  dmg/10 ticks), bypassing Creative/Spectator invulnerability and totems, exactly like vanilla's own below-world void damage.

Water evaporates in the End (bundled datapack: data/minecraft/dimension_type/the_end.json)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Added 2026-10-06.
- Water behaves in the End exactly as in the Nether: emptying a water bucket (by hand or from a dispenser) just hisses and leaves no water, breaking
  ice leaves nothing behind, and a placed wet sponge dries out at once. It's the Nether's own switch (minecraft:gameplay/water_evaporates) turned on
  for the End, not a copy of its behaviour, so anything vanilla ties to it follows along.
- Done by overriding the End's dimension type with vanilla's own definition plus that one setting, so everything else about the End is unchanged.
  Takes effect on server start. If a future Minecraft version changes the End's dimension type, this file needs updating to match, or it will hold
  the End at today's definition. Disabling the "VanillaKFC" datapack reverts it.

Beacons (BeaconListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Added 2026-10-06.
- Height: a beacon's effects reach the full height of the world, from the bottom (bedrock) to the build limit. Vanilla's reach all the way up but
  only as far below the beacon as its radius.
- Radius: a flat 10, plus the sum of the blocks in the beacon's pyramid, rounded up to a whole block - replacing vanilla's 20/30/40/50 by tier:
      Iron Block       +0.3 each
      Gold Block       +0.5 each
      Emerald Block    +0.7 each
      Diamond Block    +2.0 each
      Netherite Block  +10.0 each
  Only the layers that count toward the tier count here (9/25/49/81 blocks per layer, 164 in a full tier 4); blocks in an incomplete layer below
  them add nothing, as they add nothing to the tier. Mixed pyramids add up per block. Examples: full iron tier 1 = 10 + 2.7 = 12.7, so 13
  (vanilla 20); full iron tier 4 = 10 + 49.2 = 59.2, so 60 (vanilla 50); full diamond tier 4 = 10 + 328 = 338 (vanilla 50). (+10 base and rounding
  added 2026-10-06; it was the bare block sum at first.)
- Full netherite tier 4 (all 164 blocks netherite): the effects reach every player in that beacon's dimension, anywhere.
- Radius is measured like vanilla's - a square, not a circle: X and Z are each checked on their own, so the corners count. It's measured out from
  the beacon block's own edges to the edge of the player's hitbox, so at radius 13 a player standing in the 13th block out in X and/or Z is
  covered, and so is one just stepping over into the 14th (vanilla's hitbox overlap, 0.3 of a block).
- Everything else is vanilla: effects every 4 seconds, the same durations (9 + 2 x tier seconds) and amplifiers, the secondary effect and level II
  primary at tier 4, ambient particles, and the beam must reach the sky (any block that blocks all light, tinted glass included, stops it; bedrock
  doesn't). Players only, as in vanilla. Vanilla's own effect application is cancelled so nothing is applied twice.
- Fixed 2026-10-08 - level II never applied: choosing the primary effect again as the tier 4 secondary (e.g. Haste + Haste for Haste II) gave
  only level I. The plugin looked for that choice in the beacon's secondary effect, which Paper reports as empty whenever it matches the primary,
  so it never saw it. It now takes the level from the primary effect, which carries vanilla's own amplifier for exactly that case.
- Active beacons stay loaded: each working beacon (complete layer 1 and a clear beam) holds its chunk loaded with a plugin chunk ticket, so its
  effects keep going with nobody nearby. Like a spawn chunk, that chunk's mobs, crops and redstone keep running too. A beacon that loses its base or
  beam lets its chunk go, and picks it up again once it works. Beacon locations are saved in each world's data, so they're loaded again after a
  restart; beacons built before this existed are found as their chunks load.

No shulker boxes in ender chests (EnderChestShulkerListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Added 2026-10-12.
- A shulker box (any colour, empty or full) can no longer be put into an ender chest. Every way in is refused: placing it from the cursor,
  shift-clicking it up from the inventory, swapping it in with a number key or the off-hand key, and dragging it across a slot. The item simply
  stays where it was.
- Shulker boxes already in an ender chest are untouched: they stay there and can be taken out as normal, but can't be put back once out. Taking
  one out by swapping it with another item still works, as long as the item going in isn't itself a shulker box.
- Everything else about ender chests is vanilla.


9. LOOT TABLES
==================================================================

Fishing treasure pool (FishingLootListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Vanilla's fishing treasure pool has 6 equal-weight entries (name tag, saddle, bow, fishing rod, book, nautilus shell), making an enchanted book a
  1-in-6 (~16.7%) pick. This halves it to 1-in-12 (~8.3%): on a book catch, a coin flip either keeps it or re-rolls the real vanilla treasure table
  (rejecting further books), so the freed-up probability lands back on the other five entries in their original proportions instead of being deleted.

Fortune on ore blocks (FortuneLootListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Applies to coal, copper, diamond, emerald, gold, iron, lapis, nether gold, and nether quartz ore (+ deepslate variants), and amethyst clusters -
  every block using vanilla's minecraft:ore_drops Fortune formula. Blocks using a different bonus formula (redstone, glowstone, etc.) are untouched
  here - the crops among them are covered by CropFortuneListener below - and Silk Touch bypasses this entirely, same as vanilla.

    Fortune level   Vanilla (~uniform)              New probability table
    I               1x 66.7%, 2x 33.3%              1x 60%, 2x 40%
    II              1x 50%, 2x 25%, 3x 25%           1x 40%, 2x 50%, 3x 10%
    III             1x 40%, 2x 20%, 3x 20%, 4x 20%   1x 20%, 2x 60%, 3x 10%,
                                                      4x 10%

  Fortune levels above III (only reachable via commands) fall through to unmodified vanilla behavior.
- Lapis Ore's base drop (no Fortune) is flattened from vanilla's randomized 4-9 to a flat 1, matching every other ore in this list - Fortune scales
  the count from there using the table above, not vanilla's randomized base roll.

Fortune on crops (CropFortuneListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Fortune no longer raises crop yields at all. Breaking a mature crop with a Fortune tool drops exactly what the same break would have dropped with an
  unenchanted one, so farming scales by planting more rather than by enchanting the hoe.
- Covers the crops vanilla actually scales with Fortune: wheat and beetroot (their seeds), carrots and potatoes (the vegetable itself), nether wart,
  and melon blocks. Crops Fortune never affected (cocoa, sweet berries, pumpkin, torchflower, pitcher pod) need nothing and are not listed.
- The replacement yield is re-rolled from the broken block's own loot table, via BlockState#getDrops with a copy of the tool that has Fortune stripped
  off - there is no drop table written out here, so nothing to drift when a crop is retuned upstream, and the parts Fortune never touched (a potato's
  2% poisonous potato, for instance) keep their vanilla odds.
- Because both rolls are random, the plain re-roll can come out above what Fortune happened to produce - a Fortune I hoe draws zero wheat seeds about
  3% of the time, where the plain roll usually would not. Clamping downward only would quietly leave crops worse than vanilla, so a shortfall is
  dropped directly at the block instead.

End City treasure chests (EndCityLootListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Loot weight changes: applied post-hoc to vanilla's own generated loot - each roll's real item is kept, dropped, or duplicated with probability
  derived from newWeight/oldWeight, so untouched entries keep their exact original odds instead of being renormalized against a shared pool total.

    Entry                           Vanilla weight   New weight
    Diamond gear (8 pieces, each)   3                1
    Gold ingot                      15               20
    Iron ingot                      10               15
    Diamond                         5                7
    Emerald                         2                3

  Everything else (iron gear, saddle, horse armor, beetroot seeds, the smithing template pool) is unchanged.
- Diamond gear enchantment pool restricted: vanilla enchants the diamond (and iron) gear here from #minecraft:on_random_loot (levels 20-39), which
  includes 4 treasure enchantments a normal enchanting table could never roll - Mending, Frost Walker, and both curses. For diamond gear only, any
  treasure enchantment vanilla picked is discarded and re-rolled using only non-treasure enchantments (#minecraft:in_enchanting_table), reading each
  candidate's real weight/cost-range/item-compatibility live from the server's enchantment registry rather than a hardcoded list. Iron gear is
  unaffected and can still roll Mending, Frost Walker, or either curse.

Blaze rod drops (bundled datapack: data/minecraft/loot_table/entities/blaze.json)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Base blaze rod drop doubled. Vanilla's player-kill drop is a uniform 0-1 (avg 0.5); it's now 0, 1 or 2 with exactly 1/3 odds each (avg 1) - the
  integer uniform roll is inclusive at both ends. Looting's bonus is left as vanilla: each level adds a uniform 0-1 (avg +0.5), so averages go
  0.5/1/1.5/2 -> 1/1.5/2/2.5 for Looting 0/I/II/III. Still only drops when killed by a player, same as vanilla.
- Done as a loot-table override in the plugin's datapack (a copy of vanilla's 26.3 file with only the base range widened), so it applies wherever
  vanilla's table would. Disabling the "VanillaKFC" datapack reverts it (and the Unbreaking and blaze-fuel changes with it).

Blaze rod fuel (bundled datapack: data/minecraft/context_int_provider/cooking/time_blaze_rod.json)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- A blaze rod now smelts 16 items instead of 12: its burn time goes from 2400 to 3200 ticks. In 26.3 fuel values are data-driven, so this is a copy
  of vanilla's file with only the base number changed. Vanilla's divide-by-2 for fast-cooking blocks is kept, so a rod lasts 1600 ticks in a blast
  furnace or smoker, which cook twice as fast - also 16 items there.

Concrete powder in water (ConcretePowderListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- New mechanic: vanilla only converts concrete powder to concrete when it's a placed block touching water; the dropped/thrown item form never
  converts. A concrete powder item stack (any amount) sitting continuously in water for 3 seconds is swapped in place for the matching concrete color,
  same stack size. Leaving the water resets the wait.

Pet health display (PetHealthDisplayListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- New mechanic: any tamed pet (Wolf, Cat, Parrot, or a tamed horse/donkey/mule/llama - anything Tameable once owned) shows current/max health as an
  always-visible floating label above its name-tag height. This is a separate label (an invisible marker armor stand), not the pet's own custom name,
  so a real name tag and vanilla's default death message stay untouched - an earlier version wrote health straight into the pet's customName,
  polluting real names and death messages; any pet still carrying that pollution gets repaired to its real name (or cleared) on sight. Repositioned
  every tick to track a moving pet continuously; the health text is only re-sent when it actually changes. Removed the moment its pet dies.
- Wolf armor counter (added 2026-10-04): a wolf wearing armor gets a second, smaller line just above its health, in aqua, showing the armor's
  remaining/max durability (e.g. "98/128"). A text display scaled to 65%, since a name tag can't be resized; it follows the wolf the same way and
  disappears when the armor comes off or the wolf dies. Armor still on vanilla's 64 max is converted to the plugin's 128 on sight, so the counter
  never reads /64.
- Note: while a wolf wears armor, vanilla puts every hit on the armor in full and the wolf takes none, until the armor breaks - except damage that
  goes past wolf armor (falling, drowning, magic and the like). Unbreaking only slows how fast the armor wears; it doesn't change this.

Cat nine lives (CatLivesListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- New mechanic: cats have double vanilla's max health (10 -> 20) and nine lives. A blow that would kill a cat instead spends a life - the damage is
  cancelled, the cat is restored to full health, and the Totem of Undying sound plays. The ninth such blow is the one that sticks: a cat survives
  eight deaths and dies on the next, and the counter reads 9 down to 1 before the cat is gone.
- The count shows on its own gold floating label ("9 lives", "1 life") above the cat, 0.3 blocks above where the health label sits so the two stack
  rather than overlap. Always visible, on stray cats as well as tamed ones - deliberately independent of the health label, which is permanent once a
  cat is tamed but only look-triggered while it is stray.
- Lives live in the cat's own persistent data, not a plugin-side map, so the count survives restarts, chunk unloads and being carried between worlds.
  Cats that predate this listener start with the full nine the first time they are seen, and the doubled health is applied as a keyed attribute
  modifier so the same sweep can't double it twice.
- A revive also extinguishes the cat and grants the same brief invulnerability window an ordinary hit does. Cancelling the damage event means vanilla
  never sets that window itself, so without both a cat could burn or be hit through several lives in the space of a second.
- Exempt, and fatal however many lives remain: /kill, the void, and the world border (reviving in the last two just feeds the cat back into the same
  damage next tick). Anything that kills without raising a damage event at all - a plugin calling setHealth(0) - can't be intercepted either.

Hostile mob and passive animal health display (MobHealthDisplayListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- New mechanic: any hostile mob (Zombie, Skeleton, Creeper, Spider, Enderman, Slime, Ghast, Shulker, etc. - anything Enemy) or untamed passive animal
  (cows, sheep, pigs, chickens, wild horses - anything Animals) shows current/max health
  the same way as the pet display above, except the label only exists while a player is actually looking directly at that mob - appearing and
  disappearing with their aim rather than staying up permanently. "Looking at" requires both a precise crosshair hit on the mob's hitbox and an
  unobstructed line of sight (can't be seen through a wall the crosshair ray geometrically passes behind). Polled every tick for both the aim check
  and label position.
- Tamed animals are excluded here, since the pet display above already gives them a permanent label; without that exclusion a pet would carry two
  stacked labels. Look-triggered rather than always-visible matters more for animals than it did for hostiles: a render distance routinely holds whole
  herds and full farm pens, and permanent labels would mean an armor stand per animal, all re-positioned every tick.
- Labels are no longer saved with the world (changed 2026-10-05). They used to be, so a label left up when its chunk unloaded or the server stopped
  mid-look stayed in the world for good, invisible, with nothing ever removing it. Now they simply vanish with the chunk or on shutdown, and the next
  look makes a new one - no visible difference. Leftovers from before are removed automatically as their chunks load.

Horse health (HorseHealthListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Added 2026-10-06.
- Horses spawn with 40-60 max health instead of vanilla's 15-30. Rolled as 40 + 0-10 + 0-10, the same shape as vanilla's 15 + 0-7 + 0-8, so
  most horses land near 50 and 40 or 60 are rare. Covers wild horses, spawn eggs and /summon.
- Foals follow vanilla's inheritance, moved onto the new range: the parents' average plus a random spread (their difference plus 15% of the
  range either way, leaning to the middle), reflected back inside 40-60 if it runs past an end. Vanilla's own roll is capped at 30, so it's redone
  from the parents.
- Existing horses are brought onto the new range (added 2026-10-09): any horse still under 40 max health is scaled up in proportion rather than
  re-rolled - vanilla's 15 becomes 40, 30 becomes 60, and everything between keeps its place (22.5 -> 50) - so a horse bred to be good stays good.
  It is healed to its new full health. Happens once per horse, when its chunk loads or at startup if it's already loaded, and each batch is logged.
- Horses only - donkeys, mules, and skeleton and zombie horses are unchanged.

Tamed wolves (TamedWolfListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Max health raised from vanilla's 40 to 100, and melee damage increased 50% above vanilla's base. Applied on taming (deferred a tick so it lands
  after vanilla's own post-tame processing, since applying it same-tick was observed getting silently reverted), and reinforced by a periodic scan
  over every tamed wolf in the world - covering puppies bred from two already-tamed wolves (pre-tamed, no taming event to hook) and wolves already
  tamed before this listener loaded (e.g. an earlier session). Max health is corrected on every scan pass regardless of prior state; only the
  attack-damage multiply (unsafe to repeat) is gated behind a persistent "already buffed" tag. Wild, untamed wolves are unaffected.

Dogs drawing and finding fights (WolfGuardListener)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Added 2026-10-04.
- Retaliation: a hostile mob bitten by a tamed wolf turns on that wolf, instead of carrying on after the player while the dog chews on it (vanilla
  only did this reliably for some mobs). A mob already fighting a wolf stays on that one, so it doesn't flip between dogs. Once the dog is dead or
  out of range, the mob goes back to whoever it was after.
- Guarding: a tamed wolf that is standing (not told to sit), with nothing to attack and its owner online within 24 blocks, goes after the nearest
  hostile mob within 16 blocks of its owner that it can see. Checked twice a second. Not while the owner is in Creative or Spectator.
- Both leave out mobs vanilla wolves won't fight or can't reach: creepers and ghasts (vanilla's own rule - and a creeper chasing a dog would get
  around the rules on when a creeper may light its fuse), phantoms, vexes, blazes, wardens, and the neutral mobs (endermen, piglins, zombified
  piglins), so a dog never starts a fight the player didn't.


VERSION NOTES
==================================================================
- Targets Paper API 26.3 (pinned to 26.3.build.16-alpha; Paper had published no stable 26.3 build as of 2026-09-19, so this should be re-pinned when
  one ships). plugin.yml declares api-version 26.3, so the plugin will not load on an older server. Requires Java 25, as Paper 26.x does.
- Crop-growth timings quoted above come from a Monte-Carlo simulation calibrated to vanilla's random-tick rate (3/4096 per block per tick) and its 1/6
  growth roll for a solid field, with the chunk held loaded throughout - not from measured in-game play.
- Vanilla comparison numbers above use long-stable Minecraft values (durability, saturation, attack damage/speed, follow
  range, explosion radius, step height, jump strength, the Unbreaking formula, and the ~1-in-100,000 lightning roll) that have held constant across
  many versions.
- The strongest enchant folded into each of the seven biomes' Journeyman pools is verified directly against this Minecraft version's actual
  trade_rebalance data (extracted from the server jar). Swamp is the only intentional deviation (Mending -> Thorns II); the other six match vanilla's
  own values exactly.
- Likewise, the fishing/Fortune/End City vanilla baselines in section 9, and the Y=127 nether roof ceiling in section 8, are all read directly from
  this version's actual loot table and noise settings data (extracted from the server jar), not guessed.
